## 21143 Gingerbread Cookie

Winter Veil in Lordaeron smelled of these, every kitchen on the street at once, or I believe it did. I remember the smell so clearly that I sometimes catch it in the Undercity in summer, which cannot be true. Holiday spices and an egg, worked together and baked until the edges darken and the middles still give. They harden as they cool. I bake them every Winter Veil for the children of the zeppelin crews. They do not ask why I bake so many.

## 8604 Herb Baked Egg

An egg is the first thing I teach, because it tells you everything by its smell before it tells you anything by its taste. When the kitchen smells of warm spice and nothing else, it is done. When it smells of anything sharper, start again rather than argue with it. The egg is always right. My students think an egg is beneath them. An egg has embarrassed more of them than any boar.

## 1270632 Critter Cookie

Flour and nothing else, baked into small biscuits for the creatures that follow adventurers about. I make them for the rats of the Undercity who have earned my respect, which is three of them. Work the flour lightly and bake the biscuits pale. They smell of warm bread and nothing more, which is just as well, because a pet that learns to like spice will never leave you alone.

## 1270633 Skywall Souffle

Eggs and a little flour, the whites beaten until they stand, folded in, and baked without once opening the door. You cannot see a souffle rise from outside the oven, but you can smell it: the smell changes from wet egg to warm bread at the moment it peaks. Take it out then, and carry it fast. A souffle is a held breath. It will not wait for the guest to finish talking.

## 2542 Goretusk Liver Pie

A farm pie from the south, and we made one very like it in Lordaeron, with pig's liver, on the days the butcher had been. I can see the pie dish. It was blue, with a chip on the rim, and it sat on the windowsill to cool, and I do not know whose window it was. The goretusk's liver is strong and dark. Rinse it in cold water before you cook it, to calm the bitterness, then season it with mild spice and give it a slow oven. Liver cooked fast smells of iron and turns grey. Liver cooked slowly smells of something rich and gentle, like warm earth after rain, and that is when it is ready. My students make it for the living who pass through, and the living eat it as if it were familiar. Perhaps it is, to them.

## 1250157 Breakfast Omelette

An egg and a great deal of mild spice, more than seems sensible, from a recipe found in a condor's nest in Redridge. I have made it as written. It smells of morning and of a spice market, and of a kitchen with a stone floor that I can almost place. Beat the egg until it is one colour, pour it into a hot pan and draw the edges in as they set. Fold it soft. The living who eat it say it wakes them up. The deathguards eat it to remind themselves what mornings were.

## 3377 Gooey Spider Cake

Spider legs, the meat drawn out and pressed into cakes with hot spice, fried until the outside crisps and the middle stays soft. The goo is meant to stay in the middle. If it leaks into the pan you will hear it hiss and smell it scorch, and the cake is lost. Fry them at a steady heat, not a fierce one. I make them by the tray. Spiders are the one thing the Undercity can always offer a guest.

## 3376 Curiously Tasty Omelet

A raptor egg and hot spice. The egg is richer than a hen's and sets more slowly, so be patient with the middle. Beat it hard, cook it fast, fold it soft. The curious part, the living say, is how good it is. I believe them, because they go quiet and then look at the plate as if it owed them an explanation. I make it for my students on their first good day.

## 1250155 Giant Scrambled Eggs

Giant eggs and hot spice, stirred over a low heat until they set in soft curds. It takes a long time and a steady hand, and it smells, all the while, of something rich and warm, though there is nothing in the pan but egg and spice. When the smell sharpens, stop. The eggs are about to go too far. I feed these to the deathguards after the night watch, and they eat them standing at the counter, still in their armour.

## 15933 Monster Omelet

A giant egg feeds a table, and in the Undercity a table is whoever is standing nearest the stove. The eggs come from the great birds of the north, from Felwood and Winterspring, carried down by traders who have learned not to say where they found them. Beat the egg with soothing spice until it is smooth and pale, then pour it into the widest pan you own over a moderate heat and draw the edges in as they set. The smell is the guide: warm and round at first, then nutty at the edges. Nutty is the moment to fold. Brown is too late. Carry it to the table with both hands. I have watched a whole patrol of deathguards divide one of these in silence, with great care, as if it were treasure. For a moment, it was. Then they asked for another, which was the best part.

## 1225760 Sunrise Omelette

Giant eggs and holy salts, which I was warned would sting me and which do not. Cook it slowly, so the yolk stays bright as morning. The smell is clean and simple, egg and salt and the pan. I cook it at the hour the living call dawn, which down here means nothing, out of a habit I do not remember forming. Someone always comes in for it. I serve it on a white plate, so the yolk can show off.

## 1294014 Sweetpaw Jam

Berries from Hyjal, cooked down slowly with soothing spice until a drop on a cold plate wrinkles when you push it. The smell is the best part, and I can tell you that with some authority: dark fruit and warm spice, so sweet it is almost loud. The bears raid the bushes for these berries, the recipe says. I would too, if I were a bear.

## 22761 Runn Tum Tuber Surprise

The recipe comes from an imp in the ruins of Dire Maul who leads anyone who wants it on a long and stupid chase. One of my students made the chase and came back with the recipe, two new scars and a great deal to say about imps. The runn tum tuber grows in the shadows of those ruins, and it is the finest root I know, earthy and sweet. Peel it, slice it thick and roast it slowly with soothing spice until the edges darken and the middle gives to a fork. You will smell when it is right: like chestnuts on a street corner in winter. That is not my memory, it is my student's, who described it so well that I have borrowed it. The deathguards like it because it fills them. The living like it because it tastes, they tell me, of somewhere safe. I make a great deal of it.

## 24801 Smoked Desert Dumplings

The Cenarion Circle teaches this in Silithus to those who have proven themselves in the desert. The Horde's soldiers came back with it among their scars. Sandworm meat is dense and pale and full of sand. Wash it until the water runs clear, chop it fine with soothing spice, and roll it into small dumplings, pressed tight so they hold together in the smoke. Then smoke them slowly over a low fire. The smoke flavours the outside while the steam cooks the middle, and the smell moves from raw to sweet to something like toasted grain. When it reaches the grain, they are done. I cannot tell you how they taste. I can tell you that a soldier who had lost three friends in that desert ate a plate of them in my kitchen and wept. Then he asked for the recipe, and I gave it to him.
