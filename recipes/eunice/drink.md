## 1249957 Peace Tea

Peacebloom steeped in spring water until the water turns the colour of straw. Do not boil it, or it smells of hay and nothing else. Steep it, and it smells of the edge of a field in the evening, behind a house I can no longer place, which is still the best smell I know. The Frostmane trolls brew it, the recipe says. I make it for the apothecaries, who need peace more than anyone and get less of it.

## 1249963 Venomous Smoothie

Spring water, mild spice and a small venom sac, crushed and strained so that only the sharpness remains. The apothecaries checked my first batch. They pronounced it safe for the living with a disappointment I chose not to remark on. Shake it hard and serve it cold. The living say it bites the tongue. I take their word for it, and I watch them reach for a second glass.

## 21144 Egg Nog

Winter Veil, and somebody's uncle making egg nog in a great bowl, too strong, every year. I cannot see his face, but I can hear him singing. An egg, ice cold milk, holiday spirits and holiday spices, beaten until pale and thick. The spirits go in last and with restraint, which no uncle has ever shown. It smells of nutmeg and warm wood and a little of trouble. Serve it cold and keep an eye on the bowl.

## 9513 Thistle Tea

Swiftthistle steeped in spring water until it turns green-gold, then strained. Every rogue in the Undercity drinks it before work, and the Undercity has a great many rogues. They come to my counter at strange hours and pay in exact coins. Do not steep it too long. It turns bitter, and the rogues, who like it bitter, will drink it anyway and leave without a word.

## 1249958 Royal Tea

Mageroyal steeped in warm milk until the milk turns faintly violet. In Lordaeron it was served at the court, or so we said in the kitchens, the way kitchens say things about the court. I never saw a king drink it. I am not sure I ever saw a king. Warm the milk gently and never boil it, or it forms a skin. A skin on royal tea would once have cost a cook her place. Now it costs her nothing, and I still lift it off.

## 1249964 Slimy Smoothie

Ice cold milk, mild spice and spider ichor, shaken until the ichor gives up and mixes in. It smells faintly of almonds and strongly of spider. Serve it at once. If it stands, it separates, and a separated slimy smoothie is a thing nobody should have to see. The apothecaries drink these by the jug while they work, which tells you something about the apothecaries, and I will not say what.

## 1249965 Mrrggl Smrrthle

I will not attempt the name. Melon juice, hot spice and a murloc fin, shaken until the fin gives its body to the juice. It smells of a melon that has been to the seaside and come back changed. The murlocs of Silverpine would be honoured, I think, or furious. With murlocs it is hard to tell, and the noise is the same. I serve it to the brave, and to the very bored.

## 1249959 Root Tea

Liferoot, washed and sliced, steeped in warm melon juice until the juice goes dark and smells of earth and summer at once. Strain it carefully, because a root left too long makes the tea taste of the ground. I cannot taste it, but I can smell the ground arriving, a heavy, wet note under the sweetness. Lift the root out before it comes. The living notice. They always notice the things I cannot.

## 13028 Goldthorn Tea

One goldthorn makes four cups, which tells you how strong it is. Steep it in water just off the boil until it turns the colour of old brass, then strain it hard. The smell is sharp and bright, like a sword being polished. Every deathguard on the walls drinks this, and none of them need to. They cannot taste it any better than I can. I think they like the warmth of the cup in their hands. I have never asked.

## 1249966 Calcified Smoothie

Sweet nectar, hot spice and a large fang ground to powder. The fang makes the drink chalky and thick, like milk left in the sun. Strain it well, twice, because grit in a drink is the one thing nobody forgives. The living who fight for a living drink it in the mornings. The deathguards, who fight for a living and do not live, drink it out of solidarity.

## 1249960 Triage Tea

Sweet nectar and fadeleaf, a tea for the wounded, though fadeleaf is what rogues chew to slip out of sight. Perhaps the wounded would like to slip out of sight too. Warm the nectar until it steams, steep the fadeleaf and strain it before it turns bitter. I have not been sold this recipe. I have made it from description, for the apothecaries' patients, who did not complain, but then they rarely can.

## 1249967 Spicy Smoothie

Moonberry juice, soothing spice and a small flame sac, pierced and squeezed into the glass. The flame sac does the heating and the spice does the apologising. Shake it and drink it fast. I cannot tell you how hot it is. I can tell you that I once watched an orc drink one and then stand very still for a long time, looking at the ceiling, as if it had insulted him.

## 1249961 Sunny Tea

Sungrass steeped in moonberry juice until the drink turns gold. It smells of hay in the afternoon and of a warm wall in the sun. There is no sun in the Undercity. I make this tea when my students have gone quiet and pale and begun to forget what the surface is like, and I put it in their hands, and it reminds them. That is not in the recipe. It ought to be.

## 1249968 Wicked Smoothie

Morning glory dew, a great deal of soothing spice and a wicked claw, ground fine. The claw gives it a dark, bitter edge the spice cannot quite smooth away. It smells of crushed flowers and of something that would like to bite you. Serve it cold. The young deathguards drink it on a dare. The old ones drink it because they have stopped taking dares, and like it anyway.

## 1249962 Sage's Tea

Mountain silversage in morning glory dew, two things that are hard to find, steeped together into a tea that smells of cold air and stone. Warm the dew gently, steep the silversage until the drink turns pale, and strain it. The living say it tastes of nothing much, which the apothecaries believe sharpens the mind. I believe it gives them an excuse to stop working for the length of a cup, and I do not argue with either belief.
