## 7751 Brilliant Smallfish

Children catch these off every dock in Tirisfal, and I buy their buckets, because a child with a fish deserves a coin. Cook them whole in a hot pan, a handful at a time. Their eyes cloud when they are done, which is the kindest kind of signal: the fish tells you itself. My students who can do nothing else can do this, and I start them here so they have something to be proud of.

## 7752 Slitherskin Mackerel

Mackerel is oily, and the oil is the point. Lay it skin down on a hot, dry pan and let its own fat cook it. The smell fills the kitchen, climbs the stairs and reaches the street, and someone always comes down to ask what it is. That is how I find most of my customers. The fish advertises itself better than I could, and it asks for no wages.

## 1247741 Spinefin Halibut

A recipe without a seller, so far, for a fish I have not seen. I am told the spinefin halibut is broad and white and armed along its back, which is how fish say they would rather not be eaten. Cook it in a thick piece, gently, until the flakes just begin to part. I write this down so that the first cook who catches one has somewhere to start. Tell me how it smelled.

## 1270635 Pincer Bites

Crab claws, cracked and picked, bound with the least flour that will hold them, and fried in small bites. The flour is a rope, not a meal. Use too much and the bites smell of bread and nothing else. Use just enough and they smell of the sea and the pan. Crab season, the recipe calls it. In my experience every season is crab season, if you live near enough to the water.

## 6499 Boiled Clams

Clams are honest. They tell you when they are done, if you keep your nose over the pot. The steam smells sweet at first, then of the sea, and that is the moment to lift them. Clams forgive nothing, and I respect that in a food. A traveller off the zeppelin once ate two bowls standing at my counter and asked whether there was a third. There was not. I make more now.

## 6415 Fillet of Frenzy

Frenzies are all teeth and temper, and their flesh is the softest thing about them. A fillet falls apart if you look at it too long. Season it lightly, lay it skin down in a hot pan, turn it once. The smell is sweet and short-lived, gone in a breath. When it fades, the fish is done. The recipe comes from Darkshore, by way of an elf who wanted nothing from me but a fair price, which is rarer than it should be.

## 7754 Loch Frenzy Delight

The cold water of the loch makes a firmer fish than the southern frenzies, and the dwarves named it a delight, which I am told is unusual for them. A pinch of mild spice, a quick fire, and serve it hot. The smell is clean and cold, like a morning by a lake. I have never been to Loch Modan. I have been to lakes. I imagine it is similar, with more shouting.

## 7753 Longjaw Mud Snapper

The mud is in the skin, not the flesh, and you can smell it the moment the fish meets the heat. Cook it whole over a moderate fire and lift the skin away before you serve it. The flesh beneath smells of nothing but fish, which is exactly what you want from a fish. The fishermen of Tirisfal eat the skin too. They say it reminds them of the river. They did not need to say.

## 7827 Rainbow Fin Albacore

Firm and dark and rich, more like meat than fish, and it should be cooked like meat: a hard sear outside and very little in the middle. The colour tells you. Pink inside is right, grey is too far. I cannot taste the difference. I can see it, and so can the face of whoever eats it. Pink earns a nod. Grey earns politeness, which is worse.

## 2544 Crab Cake

Southshore made crab cakes on every holiday, or every other holiday, and the women argued about the spice through the window of the inn, or of the bakery. I remember the arguing better than the cakes. Pick the crawler meat clean, add a pinch of mild spice, and press it together with your hands. Fry it until the outside sets. If it falls apart, press harder next time. If it bounces, press less. The arguing is optional.

## 25704 Smoked Sagefish

Sagefish come from the lakes of Hillsbrad, where my family went in summer, or where we always said we would go. The fish smells of the sage along the banks, and the smoke brings it forward. I am sure of the smell. I am less sure of the summers. Low smoke, a pinch of mild spice and an afternoon. When the flesh turns golden and smells of herbs and woodsmoke, it is done.

## 2545 Cooked Crab Claw

Crack the claw before it meets the fire, so the heat reaches the meat inside rather than steaming it in its own shell. A pinch of mild spice into the crack. A short, hot fire. You will hear the meat start to sizzle inside the shell, a small, busy sound, and when it stops, the claw is done. Pick it out with a fork. The shell goes to the apothecaries, who want everything.

## 8238 Savory Deviate Delight

Deviate fish come from the poisoned pools of the Wailing Caverns, and whoever poisoned them did not mean to make them delicious. They are, a little. Cook the fish through, with mild spice, and do not undercook it. The apothecaries are very interested in what it does to the living. I have watched. I will say only that it is the one dish my customers have ever eaten twice, to see what would happen.

## 1252566 Savory Whimsyfin Delight

A small, bright fish with a ridiculous name. The recipe turns up in Redridge in the packs of creatures that do not cook, which means someone who did cook lost it there. A pinch of mild spice, a hot pan, a moment. The smell is sweet and quick, like fruit. I cook it when the zeppelin crews bring their children down, because the name makes them laugh, and laughter is not common enough down here.

## 7755 Bristle Whisker Catfish

Catfish were what the river gave us when it gave us anything, and my mother skinned them on the back step, or my grandmother did. Skin it always. The skin smells of the riverbed, and the riverbed is not supper. The flesh beneath is white and sweet and wants a hot pan and one turn. I can still hear that knife on the step. I cannot see the hand that held it.

## 6500 Goblin Deviled Clams

Goblins put too much heat in everything, including their clams. I teach it with the heat corrected. Tangy clam meat, hot spice, a fire that is hot but not furious. The smell will tell you when to stop: when the spice stops smelling raw and starts smelling toasted, take the pan away. A clam cooked furiously turns to leather. The goblins would sell you the leather and call it chewy.

## 7828 Rockscale Cod

Its scales would blunt a cheap knife, so buy a good one before you buy the fish. Scale it outside, if you can. The scales go everywhere and are found for weeks. Then a hot pan and a single turn. The flesh comes apart in clean white flakes and smells of cold water and salt. It needs nothing. I give it nothing, and my students learn that nothing is sometimes the hardest thing to give.

## 7213 Giant Clam Scorcho

A giant clam is dense and sweet, and in Booty Bay they scorch it in hot spice for pirates. Slice it thin before the heat touches it, so the spice reaches through, then fire it fiercely and quickly. The smoke will make your eyes run. That is how you know you have used enough spice. When the smoke clears, so has the dish. Serve it to someone with a strong stomach and a short memory.

## 20916 Mithril Headed Trout

A cold mountain fish with a silver head and a fighter's temper. Cook it whole, skin on, in a hot pan, and baste it with its own fat. When the skin blisters and the eye turns white, it is done. Leave the head on. My students ask why. I tell them the fish worked hard to keep it, and the least we can do is let it come to the table.

## 25954 Sagefish Delight

The greater sagefish, grown fat on the sage along its lake, with hot spice to wake it. Sear the fillet skin side down until the skin crackles, then turn it for a breath. The smell is the delight: green and warm and peppery, all at once. I make this one when I want the kitchen to smell of something other than the city above it, which is most days, if I am honest.

## 1319313 Plated Armorfish

A fish in armour. The plates come off with a heavy knife and a good deal of patience, and the flesh beneath is firm and pale, like a lobster's. Soothing spice, a moderate heat, and no hurry. Armoured things take time to cook, as the deathguards will tell you, and they are not talking about fish. Serve it in its own plates, washed. It makes people smile.

## 18239 Cooked Glossy Mightfish

A big, rich fish from the warm south, cut into steaks thick enough to rest like meat. Soothing spice rounds the richness without hiding it. Sear the steaks hard, rest them, serve them. The smell is so rich it is almost sweet, and it carries. When I cook mightfish, the queue at my counter starts before the fish is done. It never starts that early for anything I am proud of.

## 18241 Filet of Redgill

Redgill needs nothing but a hot pan. Lay the fillet skin side down and wait until it lifts away on its own. That is the whole lesson, and it takes most students a month to learn, because waiting is harder than doing. The smell goes from raw to sweet, and the edges go from glass to white. Lift it then. It has told you everything it knows.

## 15915 Spiced Chili Crab

Tender crab meat and a great deal of hot spice, stirred constantly in a pan hot enough to make you step back. The spice must cook into the crab, not sit on it. I cannot tell you how hot it is. I can tell you the trolls eat it without blinking and the orcs eat it while pretending not to blink, and I serve both with the same straight face.

## 18238 Spotted Yellowtail

Firm and oily, from the warm waters off Tanaris, and better the less you do. A hot pan, a single turn. The smell when it is done is clean and bright, like a sea breeze that has not yet met the docks. My students want to add things. I take the spice jar away and give it back when the fish is eaten. Most of them never ask for it again.

## 18240 Grilled Squid

A moment or an hour, nothing in between. I choose the moment. Score the squid in a crosshatch, rub a little soothing spice into the cuts and lay it on the hottest grill you have. It curls as if surprised. When the edges char, it is done. The smell is sweet and smoky, and it pulls customers in from the street like a rope.

## 18242 Hot Smoked Bass

Smoked hot and close, so the fish cooks as it smokes. Rub the summer bass with hot spice and hang it just above a smoky fire. Watch the flesh: when it turns from glass to white, take it down. Bass smoked this way keeps for days. I pack it for travellers taking the zeppelin, who are always hungry and always late, and who eat it before the zeppelin leaves the tower.

## 18244 Poached Sunscale Salmon

Water that never quite boils, for a fish that would fall apart under anything harsher. Bring it to a shiver, lower the salmon in, and leave it alone. You will see it turn from bright to pale, as if it were blushing in reverse. Lift it out then. Poaching is the gentlest thing I do in a kitchen, and the one my students find hardest, because nothing happens for so long.

## 1250158 Clam Linguine

There is no noodle in it, whatever the name promises. There is zesty clam meat, a great deal of it, sliced into long ribbons and tossed in soothing spice. Cook it in a hot pan for a moment and serve it at once, tangled. It eats like noodles and smells like the sea. I do not know who invented it. I suspect a cook who had no flour and plenty of clams, and did not complain.

## 18247 Baked Salmon

A whole salmon from the cold rivers, baked with soothing spice in a moderate oven. Whole, because the skin keeps the flesh moist, like a lid on a pot. When the eye turns white and the fin pulls away at a touch, it is done. The kitchen smells of rivers and warm spice, and the apothecaries come down to borrow things they do not need, and leave with a plate.

## 18246 Mightfish Steak

A mightfish is big enough to cut into steaks like beef, and it should be cooked like beef. Hot spice to wake it, soothing spice to calm it, a hard sear on both sides and a long rest. The smell is of the sea and of a smithy at once. I serve it to the deathguards on the nights before a long patrol. They say nothing, which is what deathguards say when something is good.

## 470370 Smoked Redgill

The expedition in Silithus sells this to soldiers who need food that keeps in the sand. Redgill, smoked slowly until it is dry enough to carry and still tender enough to eat. No spice. Smoke and time do the work. I learned it from an apothecary who came back from the desert with this recipe and very little else. He said it was the only thing there that did not try to kill him.
