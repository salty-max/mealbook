## 2538 Charred Wolf Meat

The first dish of every cook, alive or not. You learn it on the road with a knife and a fire, because there is always a wolf and there is always someone hungry. Listen to it. Wolf meat hisses when it first meets the heat and falls quiet when the outside has sealed. Turn it once, at the quiet. My students want to poke it. I let them, once, and then I let them eat what they poked.

## 15935 Crispy Bat Wing

Every new cook in Tirisfal makes this, because the bats are everywhere and little else is. I have made more of them than any dish of my first life. Score the skin of the wing and lay it on a hot griddle, skin down, and leave it alone. It talks while it cooks. When the hiss turns to a crackle, turn it once and never again. Serve it to whoever is still hungry. In the Undercity, there is always someone.

## 2540 Roasted Boar Meat

In Lordaeron we roasted boar at harvest, in a pit behind the barn, or behind the inn, or behind somebody's barn. I remember the pit clearly and the barn not at all. Down here it is a plain dish for plain days. Roast the meat in pieces, never whole: a whole boar takes the day, and the people waiting for it will not. When the fat begins to smell sweet instead of sharp, it is ready.

## 6412 Kaldorei Spider Kabob

An elven recipe, from a tree so tall the night elves are said to cook in its branches. I have never been, and I am not expected. The recipe came down to me through three traders, two of them honest. Thread the spider legs on a skewer and give them a quick, fierce heat until the shells crack like twigs in a fire. That sound is the signal. After it, every moment costs you.

## 2539 Spiced Wolf Meat

I teach this on the second day, after the charred wolf, so that my students learn spice is not a correction. Rub it into the meat before the fire. When it goes on at the end, it smells of spice and of wolf, two strangers at the same table not speaking. When it goes on at the start, it smells of supper. The living tell me the difference is just as plain on the tongue.

## 6413 Scorpid Surprise

Durotar's cooks sent this north on the first zeppelins, and the Undercity took to it. The surprise is in the stinger. Cooked until it turns from glass to chalk, it loses its venom and keeps a pleasant crunch. Undercooked, it keeps the venom, and the surprise is a different one. You can hear the change. A raw stinger rattles in the pan. A done one lies still. The rogues buy them by the handful and eat them on the stairs.

## 1250156 Tasty Raptor Bites

A raptor's egg is the size of my two fists and smells, when cracked, faintly of the swamp its mother walked through. The spice is there to answer that smell, and it takes a lot of answering. Beat the egg, season it, and cook it in small rounds on a hot stone. The rounds puff when they are done. My students eat them before I can count them, which tells me the recipe works.

## 2795 Beer Basted Boar Ribs

A dwarven dish, which means it comes with a story about whose grandfather invented it, and the story changes with whoever is telling it. I take no sides. The ribs and the malt are honest whoever cooked them first. Every dwarf I have heard of bastes from the first minute and then blames the fire for what follows. Roast the ribs bare until the fat runs clear, and only then begin the malt, a little at a time, so it turns to a glaze. When it smells of burnt sugar, you have gone too far. When it smells of bread and smoke, you are exactly right. The deathguards ask for this one by name, which is more than they do for their sergeants. I make it on feast days, and on the days that need one.

## 6414 Roasted Kodo Meat

The tauren of Mulgore roast kodo the way they do most things, slowly and without fuss. I cook it the same way. Kodo is a great, slow animal, and its meat is tough until it decides otherwise. A pinch of spice, a low fire, and an afternoon. It is done when it smells of warm grass and smoke. I cannot taste it, so I asked a tauren trader, who told me mine was nearly as good as his mother's. I chose to believe the nearly was politeness.

## 8607 Smoked Bear Meat

No spice and no sauce, only smoke and time. This is food for people who are going somewhere. Cut the meat along the grain and hang it high over a low fire of green wood, where the heat never reaches and the smoke does all the work. In the morning the strips should bend before they break. I pack them for the deathguards on the road to the Sepulcher. They do not need to eat, and they take three each.

## 2541 Coyote Steak

Coyotes eat whatever the world drops, and their meat remembers every meal. It takes no spice, only a hot pan and a cook who does not apologise for it. Sear it until the edges curl and the smell turns from blood to toasted nuts, then take it off the heat and leave it alone. A coyote steak is never tender, the living tell me, chewing. It is honest, and honest food keeps a traveller on their feet for a long road.

## 3371 Blood Sausage

Every autumn the farmhouses of Lordaeron made blood sausage, and every autumn someone's grandmother knew the only proper way. Mine swore by thyme. Or sage. I was certain for years. Down here the Spider Ichor binds it where blood once did, and the Royal Apothecary Society assures me it is safe for the living, which is more than they say about most things. The living come back for seconds, the only review I trust. Poach the links before they see the pan. A sausage that splits was packed by someone in a hurry.

## 1250154 Twice-Spiced Raptor Slice

This is the only dish where I break my own rule of spice before the fire and season at the end, because raptor is lean and the first coat cooks away. I learned to smell the moment between the two. When the slices stop hissing and begin to tick, like a pan cooling, take them off and season them again while they are hot enough to drink it. My students always forget the second coat. The dish tells on them. It smells of half a recipe.

## 3370 Crocolisk Steak

Crocolisk reaches the Undercity from the Wetlands by way of people who would rather not say how. I do not ask. The meat is pale and firm, more fish than beast, and it wants a pinch of spice and very little time. Cook it once. Crocolisk heated a second time smells of the swamp, and the swamp is not what anyone ordered. Serve it at once, to whoever is standing nearest.

## 2546 Dry Pork Ribs

No sauce, only spice and a low fire and the whole of an afternoon, the way the farms cooked them at harvest. I have lost which farm. The ribs are done when the bones show a finger's width at the ends and the smell has gone from meat to something closer to toasted bread. I serve them on paper, to people who eat with their hands and do not mind who sees. In the Undercity, that is most people.

## 6418 Crispy Lizard Tail

The Barrens taught me this one, along with patience and a respect for anything that survives there. A thunder lizard's tail is solid muscle wrapped in a skin like old leather. Cook it in its own fat with hot spice until the skin crackles under a spoon. If it bends, it is not done. If it shatters, it is perfect. The orcs eat it whole. I cut it into rounds, for the sake of the furniture.

## 2549 Seasoned Wolf Kabob

Stormwind Seasoning Herbs, the packet says, and they smell exactly like a market I remember. Where that market stood, I no longer know. The wolf does not care. Thread the meat with gaps between the pieces, so the heat reaches every side, and listen for the fat to spit. When it stops spitting and starts to sing, turn the skewers. The first one goes to whoever has been waiting longest.

## 2548 Succulent Pork Ribs

Ribs and hot spice, turned every few minutes so the fat bastes the meat as it melts. The turning is the recipe. A rib left to itself burns on the bottom and sulks on top. A rib turned often comes out glossy and generous. A farmer taught me that, a neighbour, I think, with a red beard, or a red barn. I give this one to the students who think cooking is waiting. It is not. It is paying attention while you wait.

## 3397 Big Bear Steak

The bears of the south are bigger than ours, and their meat is darker, with a smell of pine and wet earth that the hot spice cuts through. Sear the steak until it smells of smoke and iron, then let it rest a long while. A big steak goes on cooking after the fire, and many a cook has ruined one by staring at it. I tell my students to walk away and count to a hundred, and to tell me afterwards how it tastes. Most reach forty.

## 6419 Lean Venison

There were deer in the woods above the farm, and my father, or my uncle, or a neighbour with a bow, brought one down each autumn. The face is gone. The smell of the meat in the pan is not. Venison is lean and proud and wants plenty of spice and very little fire. Take it off while the middle is still dark. Lean meat cooked through smells of nothing but regret.

## 15853 Lean Wolf Steak

A plain steak from a wolf's flank, with a pinch of spice and nothing to hide behind. That is what makes it a test. Sear it fast, rest it long, slice it thin across the grain. I judge it by its colour and by the face of whoever eats it, since I cannot do otherwise. I make my students cook it until they stop flinching at the hiss of the pan. It takes some of them a week. One took a year.

## 3398 Hot Lion Chops

The lions of the Barrens are lean and sweet close to the bone, so the chops stay on the bone. Hot spice, a moderate fire, and many turns. The heat is for others to judge, and they do. The orcs from the Crossroads ask for more spice every time and the trolls ask for less, so I make two pans and let them argue about who has the right one.

## 3399 Tasty Lion Steak

Lion cooked as a steak rather than chopped, with soothing spice to round the sweetness of the meat. The soothing spice smells of cloves and warm stone. Rub it in, sear the steak hard, rest it as long as you seared it. Slice it across the grain, or it fights back in the mouth. The name was written by someone who had eaten it and was too busy to find a better word.

## 1250151 Bear Brisket

My students learn to keep a fire low on this, the toughest cut of the toughest animal. Rub the brisket with hot spice and let it cook through a whole day, until it gives under a fork like wet bread. You cannot rush it. You can only watch it, and smell it change from raw iron to smoke to something rich and dark. When it smells like a winter evening, it is ready.

## 4094 Barbecued Buzzard Wing

There are more buzzards than cooks in the Barrens, and the cooks have found a use for them. The wing wants hot spice, a long low heat to soften the sinew, then a short fierce flame to blister the skin. You can hear the change. The fat stops whispering and starts to crackle. Serve them hot and in quantity. Nobody has ever eaten only one buzzard wing.

## 15863 Carrion Surprise

The trolls of the swamp call this a pie and mean it kindly. Mystery meat and hot spice, cooked until there is no doubt left about anything. I can smell what the meat was, which is more than the trolls can, and I still serve it. Cook it through, then cook it again. Whatever it was, it has had a long journey, and it deserves a firm ending.

## 15856 Hot Wolf Ribs

Red wolves from the far south, fattier than the grey ones of Silverpine, with ribs that need time to render. Hot spice and a hot fire, then patience. The fat runs clear before it runs smoky, and the moment between is the one you want. The deathguards who come back from the south bring these up with them wrapped in cloth, as if they had found treasure. For a cook, they had.

## 15855 Roast Raptor

The camps of Stranglethorn roast raptor in large pieces, and so do I, because raptor is lean and a large piece keeps its own middle moist. Hot spice on the outside, a strong fire, then pull it away from the heat to finish. The smell turns from metal to smoke to roasted nuts. Somewhere around the nuts, take it off. The raptor fought hard. Do not let it win at the table.

## 1250150 Raging Raptor Ribs

I have read this recipe but never been sold it, which makes me suspicious of it. The ribs cook low until the meat lets go of the bone, then over a fierce flame to set a thick crust of hot spice. The smoke alone made my eyes water, and nobody I fed them to asked for more spice. The crust should crack like burnt sugar under the knife. If it bends, the ribs go back over the flame.

## 1295834 Plain Ol' Paletusk

Boar, roasted on the bone with a little mild spice and nothing else, the way farm kitchens have always done it. The recipe comes from the Riverglades, from someone who clearly had hungry people waiting. Roast it until the fat crackles and the smell turns from meat to hazelnut. Do not decorate it. A plain dish is a promise that the meat is good, and a cook who adds things is admitting the meat was not.

## 21175 Spider Sausage

Spiders are the one thing the Undercity is never short of. White spider meat is sweet and delicate and takes no spice at all, which is a mercy in a city that smells of everything. Press it tight with your hands so it holds together, and fry it until the outside crisps. In the pan, listen for the crust to snap under the spoon. It is the most satisfying sound in my kitchen, and my students learn to wait for it.

## 1250153 Raptor Rouladen

I roll thin slices of raptor around hot spice and tie them with string. The rolls cook from the outside in, so keep the outer turn thin. Brown them on every side, then lower the fire and leave them. When the string darkens and smells of burnt hay, cut it away before anyone sees it. A guest will forgive a great deal, but not string. I learned that from a guest.

## 1250142 Savory Stag Sliders

The living like food they can eat with one hand, and this is it: stag chopped fine with hot spice, pressed once into small rounds and fried quickly. Press them twice and the juice runs into the pan instead of the guest. I have watched dockworkers at the zeppelin tower finish a dozen of these between two arrivals and go back to work without a word. That is the review that matters.

## 22480 Tender Wolf Steak

The wolves of the cold places live slowly and keep their fat, and their meat is the best of any wolf. Soothing spice, a short hot sear, a long rest. You will smell when it is ready: the spice warms and the meat turns sweet, like a loaf just out of the oven. My students swear they taste the difference from the first bite, and I believe them, because they stop talking. A cook who has fought a hundred stringy wolves deserves this one. I serve it to my best students.

## 1225759 Filet o' Flank

Holy salts, from the churches of the living, which made me laugh the first time I held a pouch of them. They are good salts. The Light has nothing against me in a kitchen. Rub them into the red wolf flank and leave it an hour, then cook it quickly. The salt draws the water from the meat and firms it. It does not burn me, before you ask. My students always ask.

## 1225758 Prowler Steak

Tiger, the name means, cut thick and rubbed with holy salts an hour before cooking. Tiger is a hard-working meat and the salt calms it. Then a hot iron, a short sear and a long rest. You will hear it relax on the board, a soft settling, like a dog lying down. That is when you slice. Not before. A tiger that has not settled will not settle at the table either.

## 1250144 Bat Hachee

Poor farmers made this stew, chopped meat cooked long and slow. In Lordaeron it was beef, when there was beef, or I believe it was. Down here it is bat. Chop the wings fine, add the soothing spice, and give it a low fire for an evening, until the sinew softens and the kitchen smells of the spice and of something like home. My students call it bat hash. I call it supper.

## 1250147 Bear Bruscitti

My students remember this one by its smell: big bear meat in thin slices, layered with soothing spice and roasted slowly until the layers press together into one. Slice the meat cold, or it tears. The smell when it is done is the best part, dark and sweet and close to burnt sugar. The name means nothing to me, nor to anyone I have asked. Whoever named it was showing off. Whoever cooked it first was not.

## 1250152 Prehistoric Pulled Raptor

The deathguards love this one because it keeps. The raptor cooks for most of a day over the lowest fire I can keep, with plenty of soothing spice, until it falls apart at the touch of a fork. Pull it with two forks, never a knife. It can be eaten from a bowl while walking, which suits them. Prehistoric, it calls itself. So are some of my students' manners.

## 1250143 Soaring Pamplona

My students who are proud of their knife get this one: buzzard wings, boned and beaten flat, rolled around soothing spice and tied. It is slow, fiddly work, and I check every roll before it goes in the oven. Roast it gently and slice it in rounds. The spiral inside tells me everything about the cook who rolled it. A tight spiral, a careful hand. A loose one, someone who was thinking about lunch.

## 1250148 Steaming Stag Steak

I rub the stag deep with soothing spice, sear it on a very hot iron and serve it at once, while it still steams. The steam carries the smell, and the smell is half the meal for anyone who can taste and all of it for me. Do not let it wait. A steak that stops steaming has started to cool, and a cold steak is a promise someone broke on the way from the kitchen.

## 1250149 Swiftstrike Steak

Give the tiger meat its soothing spice, the hottest fire you can make and the shortest time you can bear. The name is a hunter's. The method is a cook's. Strike fast, then rest long, and the resting is where everyone fails, because the smell is wonderful and the fork is right there. I make my students sit on their hands, and I mean it. I learned the method from a hunter who could not wait either.

## 1250146 Flank au Poivre

I looked for the pepper in this recipe and found none, whatever the name says, only red wolf flank and soothing spice, which does pepper's work well enough. Sear the flank hard, rest it while the pan cools, then pour its juices back over it. The smell when they meet the warm meat is the whole point of the dish. I serve it to the apothecaries when I want something from them, and it works.

## 25659 Dirge's Kickin' Chimaerok Chops

A goblin in Gadgetzan cooks chimaerok with rocket fuel, and the Horde's travellers carry the recipe home like a dare. Mine came from a tauren who had lost his eyebrows and kept his appetite. Chimaerok tenderloin is a long way to go for a piece of meat, the islands off Feralas, and it is worth every step. Deeprock salt, hot spice, and the goblin rocket fuel, which goes on the fire, never in the food. Stand back further than seems polite. The flare lasts a breath and leaves a crust no honest coal could make. The smell fills the whole Apothecarium, and the apothecaries come out of their rooms to complain and stay to eat. I do not ask what they think of it. I count the plates.
