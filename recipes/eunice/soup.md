## 1255241 Cleansing Stew

Felbloom, cleansed by the druids so that it no longer does what felbloom does. I keep my distance from it anyway, out of habit. Stag meat and a giant egg give the stew its strength, the egg beaten in at the end so the broth turns silky. It smells of green things and warm broth and, faintly, of something burnt that is no longer there. I cook it for those who come back from Felwood looking as if the forest came with them.

## 6416 Strider Stew

Strider and apple, the first stew of the Barrens. The apple goes in with the meat and cooks away to nothing, and when you can no longer find it in the pot, the stew is done. Strider is a stubborn bird. The apple softens it. I make this for orcs who miss home and for trolls who miss a home that is gone, and they all eat it the same way, very fast.

## 2543 Westfall Stew

A farmer's stew from the south, made from what the land gives when it gives very little: vulture, a goretusk's snout and a murloc's eye, which I will not apologise for. We had a stew like it in Lordaeron, made from whatever the fields and the river spared. I remember the pot. I remember it was black and heavy, and that someone always burned their hand on it. Whose hand, I could not tell you any more. The eye gives the broth its body, so do not leave it out to spare a squeamish guest. Squeamish guests can go hungry. Let it simmer until the vulture gives up, which is longer than you think. Travellers from the south tell me the farms there stand empty now. The stew outlived the farms, as stews tend to.

## 6501 Clam Chowder

Somebody in Southshore sold chowder from a door near the harbour, and the smell carried along the quay. I remember the smell exactly and the door not at all. Warm the milk gently, add the clam meat and a pinch of mild spice, and keep it below a simmer. Milk that boils splits, and you can smell it happen, a sour edge on the steam. If you smell it, start again.

## 6417 Dig Rat Stew

The Barrens taught me this, and the Undercity took to it, because we are not short of rats either, though ours are not fit to eat and the dig rats of the Barrens are. Stew them long, until the meat falls from the small bones, and lift the bones out with patience and a spoon. My students make faces. I make them eat it. Then they make it themselves, for the next students, and the faces continue.

## 3372 Murloc Fin Soup

Murloc fins and hot spice, simmered until they melt into the broth and leave it thick and rich, like bone stock. The smell is the sea at low tide, which sounds unpleasant and is not, once the spice gets to it. The murlocs along the coast of Silverpine would object, if they knew. They object to most things, loudly, and I have long since stopped listening.

## 2547 Redridge Goulash

Spider and condor, stewed together. The condor is tough and the spider is not, so the condor goes in first and the spider much later. Put them in together and you will smell the spider overcook, a sharp, sour note, long before the condor is anywhere near done. It is a dish of the south, from people with more spiders than they wanted. I understand those people very well.

## 3373 Crocolisk Gumbo

Tender crocolisk meat and hot spice, cooked down until the broth is thick and dark enough to coat a spoon. The colour tells you, and so does the smell: when it goes from swamp to toasted nut, you are there. Stir from the bottom, always. I cannot taste a gumbo, but I can smell a burnt one from the other end of the Undercity, and so, I am told, can everyone else.

## 24418 Heavy Crocolisk Stew

Crocolisk by the potful, soothing spice, and a stew that a spoon can stand in. Stir it from the bottom, often, because a stew this thick sticks and burns where you cannot see, and the smell gives you away only when it is too late. The soldiers of the marshes eat it out of tin bowls in the rain, I am told. My deathguards eat it out of tin bowls in the dry. Tin bowls are the same everywhere.

## 15861 Jungle Stew

Tiger meat, spring water and apples, the stew of Stranglethorn. Brown the meat first, then add the water and the apples and let it simmer until the fruit has gone into the broth. The smell turns sweet, then deep, then sweet again. The second sweetness means it is ready. Trolls taught it to me, Darkspears on their way somewhere warmer. They said it tastes of home. I took their word for it.

## 15865 Mystery Stew

Mystery meat stewed in dwarven stout, and to me there is no mystery about either. I can smell exactly what the meat was. I can smell the brewery the stout came from, and what was spilled on its floor. Cook it long and gently, and never let it boil, or the stout turns sour and you will smell that from the stairs. Skim the pot as it simmers. The stout makes the meat dark and soft and gives the broth a bitterness the living seem to like a great deal. The apothecaries, who should know better, eat it in quantity and refuse to say what they think is in it. I have watched a whole table of them argue over it for an evening. I have stopped telling them. They enjoy it more not knowing, and so, I think, does everyone.

## 3400 Soothing Turtle Bisque

Turtle and soothing spice, simmered for hours, then worked through a sieve until it is smooth as cream. The sieve is the recipe. The smell while it simmers is warm and round, like cloves in a closed room, and it slows people down when they pass my kitchen. I like a dish that slows people down. Down here, everyone is in such a hurry to be somewhere they have already been.

## 1295805 Briny Seafood Stew

A stew of the shore: crab, murloc eyes and hot spice. The eyes thicken the broth, so do not leave them out to spare anyone's feelings. The crab goes in at the end, so it stays sweet. The smell is the sea before anyone has tidied it, salt and weed and something still alive. My students hold their noses the first time. The second time they ask for a bowl.

## 15906 Dragonbreath Chili

Mystery Meat goes into this, and I would rather not say what my nose tells me about it. The Barrens taught me this dish, not Lordaeron, which never ate anything that fought back this hard. I cannot tell you how hot this chili is. I can tell you that the toasted spices sting my eyes, which is the one part of it I still feel. The last tauren who tried mine set his spoon down very carefully and asked for water in a voice I had not heard from him before. Pierce the flame sac before it goes in, or it will pierce itself. I make it when a zeppelin crew comes in from a long, cold run. Make it for people you like, and warn them. Then watch their faces. The dish is done when they stop talking.

## 15910 Heavy Kodo Stew

The kodos of Desolace grow heavier than those of the Barrens, though the land gives them little, and their meat is the densest I know. The tauren taught me this stew, and I have never changed a thing, because there is nothing to change. Heavy kodo meat, soothing spice and spring water, and a whole day over a low fire. You will smell it turn: raw, then grassy, then deep and brown, like a field after rain. Somewhere in the evening the meat comes apart in threads at the touch of a spoon. That is the moment, and no other. I make it on the coldest days for the deathguards on the walls above, and I carry it up myself. They do not need it. They line up anyway, and they are very quiet while they eat.

## 20626 Undermine Clam Chowder

A goblin chowder from Tanaris: zesty clam meat, hot spice and ice cold milk, warmed together without ever boiling. The goblins eat it scalding, which tells you everything about goblins. Keep the heat low and watch the steam. When it smells of the sea and of warm spice, and not yet of scorched milk, ladle it out. The goblin who sells it keeps one copy at a time. I bought it, so he has had to write himself another.

## 18243 Nightfin Soup

A snapper caught at night, and spring water, and nothing else. Simmer the fish gently until the flesh falls from the bones, then lift the bones out with care. The broth should be clear enough to read through. It smells of very little, which is the point: a clean, quiet soup for people who have had a loud day. I make it for myself at the end of the evening, and I do not mind that I cannot taste it.

## 1250145 Savory Turtle Stew

I stew this turtle and never strain it, because it is a farm stew and not a lord's bisque. We made it by a river in Lordaeron, I am nearly sure, though I could not tell you which. Turtle and plenty of soothing spice, simmered until the meat comes apart. Leave it thick. The texture is the point, and the smell, round and warm and slow, is the reason people stay at the counter after they have finished. I have stopped asking them to move. They will, when the bowl is empty, and not before.

## 18245 Lobster Stew

Darkclaw lobster in spring water, and that is all. Lower the lobster in whole and lift it out the moment the shell turns red. Take the meat from the shell and warm it in the broth. Never boil it twice. The smell of a lobster going too far is sharp and unmistakable, like hot copper, and once you have smelled it you will never let it happen again. I let it happen once. That was enough.

## 470359 Darkclaw Bisque

Holy water from Stratholme, which the apothecaries handed me with a straight face. I went to Stratholme once, alive, for a market or a wedding, and I remember the bells and the bread and nothing else of it. Now its water makes a bisque. Darkclaw lobster, simmered in it until the shell turns red, then sieved smooth. The water is very soft and very clean, and it does not burn me. It lets the lobster speak. I listen.
