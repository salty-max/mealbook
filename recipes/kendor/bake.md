## 21143 Gingerbread Cookie

A Winter Veil biscuit, sold for a few weeks each year by smiling merchants in red, and made by children who eat half of it before it reaches the oven. The holiday spices are the whole of it: warm, sweet and sharp, and they must be fresh, because stale spice tastes of last year. An egg binds it. Take them out while the middles are still soft to the touch, because they go on hardening as they cool. A biscuit baked hard in the oven is a weapon by morning.

## 8604 Herb Baked Egg

Every cook in this city believes he can bake an egg. Most are wrong in the same way: too hot, too long, and in a hurry to be done with it. It is the first dish I teach and the one I judge a cook by. Season the white, never the yolk. The yolk is already perfect and does not want your opinion. Tomas in Goldshire makes a perfectly respectable one. I taught him.

## 1270632 Critter Cookie

A biscuit made for the small animals that follow adventurers about, and made, I note, of nothing but flour, which tells you how often the small animals are consulted. The flour is worked with a light hand and baked until pale gold. The critters adore them. I have seen a cat eat one. The cat looked at me afterwards as though it expected more, which is the only review a pet has ever given.

## 1270633 Skywall Souffle

A souffle is an egg persuaded to stand up, and the Skywall souffle, named for some high place I have not visited, stands up higher than most. The whites are beaten until they hold a peak and folded into the yolks and a little flour with a light hand, and then the oven door stays shut. That is the rule. Open the door and it falls, and it will not rise again for you or anyone. A souffle is the only dish that sulks permanently.

## 2542 Goretusk Liver Pie

Goretusk liver pie is the pride of the Westfall farms, or was, when there were Westfall farms to be proud of. A farmer's wife on the coast road taught it to half the province, and I sell it here with a few corrections she has not yet travelled to Stormwind to dispute. The liver of a goretusk is strong, dark and rich, and it must be rinsed in cold water before it is cooked, to calm its bitterness. Then mild spice, and an oven that never hurries. A hot oven makes liver grey and grainy, with a taste of iron. A slow one leaves it soft and sweet and tasting of the farm it came from. The crust is a matter of opinion. The liver is not. When the pie comes out, let it rest until it stops whispering. Then cut it, and then you may be proud.

## 1250157 Breakfast Omelette

A breakfast omelette, with an egg and a frankly alarming quantity of mild spice. Whoever wrote this recipe woke up hungry and angry. The trick of any omelette is heat and speed: whisk the egg smooth, give it a pan already hot, and keep a fork moving through it while it sets. Fold it while the middle is still soft. The recipe was found in Redridge, in the nest of a condor, which explains the anger, if not the spice.

## 3377 Gooey Spider Cake

I sell a recipe called gooey spider cake, and I sell it with a straight face, because it is very good. Gooey spider legs, picked of their meat, which is mixed with hot spice, pressed into cakes and fried until there is a crust worth the name and the inside is still soft. The goo is in the name and should stay in the middle. A cake that leaks has been fried too hot. The people of Duskwood eat these by the dozen, which is the bravest thing I know about Duskwood.

## 3376 Curiously Tasty Omelet

Curiously tasty is the most honest name in my catalogue, because everyone who eats it is curious why it is so good. It is a raptor egg, which is richer than a hen's by a distance, and hot spice. A fierce whisk, a quick pan, a gentle fold. A raptor's egg is slow to set at the centre, and a cook who rushes it serves the centre raw. The curiosity is earned. The omelette is mine, by right of improvement.

## 1250155 Giant Scrambled Eggs

Scrambled eggs are the first test of a cook's patience, and giant scrambled eggs are the same test in a larger pan. Giant eggs and hot spice, beaten together and stirred over a low heat, slowly, always, until they gather into soft curds. A hot pan makes them rubber. A gentle pan makes them silk. Most cooks cannot bear to stir for that long. Most cooks should not be allowed near a giant egg.

## 15933 Monster Omelet

A monster omelette is made from a giant egg, and a giant egg is enough to feed a table of six or one orc. Giant eggs are the trade of the far north, and the recipe is sold in Felwood and Winterspring by merchants who keep their suppliers to themselves. Beat it with the soothing spice for longer than your arm likes. Then set the widest pan you own over a moderate heat, and fold the set edges towards the centre with a fork. The temptation is to rush it, because it is large and the guests are watching. Resist. A monster omelette cooked fast is brown outside and raw within, and nobody forgives that. Fold it when the top is barely set and let the heat finish the middle on its way to the table. Then carry it in with both hands, and enjoy the silence.

## 1225760 Sunrise Omelette

An omelette of giant eggs seasoned with holy salts, which someone decided was a sunrise. It is the colour: a very bright yolk, a pale white, and a pinch of salt that the clergy blessed and the merchants priced accordingly. Keep the heat gentle, or the yolk dulls. A sunrise cooked fast turns to noon, and nobody writes poems about noon. I serve it to priests, who appreciate the joke, and to paladins, who do not.

## 1294014 Sweetpaw Jam

Jam from the berries of Hyjal, which grow on the slopes of a mountain the druids call sacred and which is, above all, very cold. Berries and soothing spice, reduced over a gentle fire. To test it, chill a saucer and drop a little jam on it: if it wrinkles when pushed, it is ready. Too runny, and you have made sauce. Too stiff, and you have made a brick. Sweetpaw, they call it, after the bears who raid the bushes. The bears are right about the berries.

## 22761 Runn Tum Tuber Surprise

The recipe for this dish is carried by an imp in the ruins of Dire Maul. He parts with it only after a merry chase through halls full of very angry satyrs. I know this because a pupil of mine made the chase and returned with the recipe, a limp and an opinion of imps that I share. The tuber grows in the shadow of those ruins and tastes, cooked well, like the best root you never knew existed: earthy, sweet and faintly smoky. Peel it, cut it thick so the outside caramelises before the inside is done, and give it soothing spice and a slow oven until a fork goes in without resistance. The surprise is that anything this good comes from a place that bad. I teach the recipe gladly. I do not teach the chase, and I have advised my pupil never to repeat it.

## 24801 Smoked Desert Dumplings

This recipe is not sold. It is taught by the Cenarion Circle's cooks in Silithus, and only to those who survived a desert that wants them dead. I have not been to Silithus. I do not need to have been to know that sandworm meat is a challenge: dense, pale and fond of sand in every crease. Wash it past the point where the water runs clear, because sand hides in what looks clean. Mince it with soothing spice and shape small dumplings, pressed hard so they keep their shape in the smoke, and set them on a rack high above a low fire. The outside takes the smoke and the middle takes the steam, and when they are done they are firm, fragrant and entirely free of sand, which is the true achievement. The Circle guards it jealously, and I respect them for it. I have guarded worse for less.
