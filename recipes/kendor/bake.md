## 21143 Gingerbread Cookie

A Winter Veil biscuit, sold for a few weeks each year by smiling merchants in red, and made by children who eat half of it before it reaches the oven. The holiday spices are the whole of it: warm, sweet and sharp, and they must be fresh, because stale spice tastes of last year. An egg binds it. Bake the biscuits until the edges darken and the middles still give. They harden as they cool. A biscuit baked hard in the oven is a weapon by morning.

## 8604 Herb Baked Egg

Every cook in this city believes he can bake an egg. Most are wrong in the same way: too hot, too long, and in a hurry to be done with it. It is the first dish I teach and the one I judge a cook by. Season the white, never the yolk. The yolk is already perfect and does not want your opinion. Tomas in Goldshire makes a perfectly respectable one. I taught him.

## 1270632 Critter Cookie

A biscuit made for the small animals that follow adventurers about, and made, I note, of nothing but flour, which tells you how often the small animals are consulted. The flour is worked with a light hand and baked until pale gold. I am told the critters adore them. I have seen a cat eat one. The cat looked at me afterwards as though it expected more, which is the only review a pet has ever given.

## 1270633 Skywall Souffle

A souffle is an egg persuaded to stand up, and the Skywall souffle, named for some high place I have not visited, stands up higher than most. Eggs and a little flour, the whites beaten until they hold a peak, folded in gently and baked without once opening the oven door. That is the rule. Open the door and it falls, and it will not rise again for you or anyone. A souffle is the only dish that sulks permanently.

## 2542 Goretusk Liver Pie

Goretusk liver pie is the pride of the Westfall farms, or was, when there were Westfall farms to be proud of. A farmer's wife on the coast road taught it to half the province, and I sell it here with a few corrections she has not yet travelled to Stormwind to dispute. The liver of a goretusk is strong, dark and rich, and it must be soaked in cold water before it is cooked, to draw out its bitterness. Then a pinch of mild spice and a slow oven. Liver cooked fast turns grey and grainy and tastes of iron. Liver cooked slowly turns soft and sweet and tastes of the farm it came from. The crust is a matter of opinion. The liver is not. When the pie comes out, let it rest until it stops whispering. Then cut it, and then you may be proud.

## 1250157 Breakfast Omelette

A breakfast omelette, with an egg and a frankly alarming quantity of mild spice. Whoever wrote this recipe woke up hungry and angry. The trick of any omelette is heat and speed: a hot pan, the egg beaten until it is one colour, and a fork drawing the edges in as they set. Fold it while the middle is still soft. The recipe was found in Redridge, in the nest of a condor, which explains the anger, if not the spice.

## 3377 Gooey Spider Cake

I sell a recipe called gooey spider cake, and I sell it with a straight face, because it is very good. Gooey spider legs, the meat drawn out and mixed with hot spice, pressed into cakes and fried until the outside crisps and the inside stays soft. The goo is in the name and should stay in the middle. A cake that leaks has been fried too hot. The people of Duskwood eat these by the dozen, which is the bravest thing I know about Duskwood.

## 3376 Curiously Tasty Omelet

Curiously tasty is the most honest name in my catalogue, because everyone who eats it is curious why it is so good. It is a raptor egg, which is richer than a hen's by a distance, and hot spice. Beat it hard, cook it fast and fold it soft. A raptor's egg sets more slowly than a hen's, so be patient with the middle. The curiosity is earned. The omelet is mine, by right of improvement.

## 1250155 Giant Scrambled Eggs

Scrambled eggs are the first test of a cook's patience, and giant scrambled eggs are the same test in a larger pan. Giant eggs and hot spice, beaten together and stirred over a low heat, slowly, always, until they set in soft curds. A hot pan makes them rubber. A low pan makes them silk. Most cooks cannot bear to stir for that long. Most cooks should not be allowed near a giant egg.

## 15933 Monster Omelet

A monster omelet is made from a giant egg, and a giant egg is enough to feed a table of six or one orc. The eggs come from the great birds of the north, sold in Felwood and Winterspring by people who have learned not to ask where they were gathered. Beat the egg with soothing spice until it is smooth and pale, then cook it in the widest pan you own, over a moderate heat, drawing the edges in as they set. The temptation is to rush it, because it is large and the guests are watching. Resist. A monster omelet cooked fast is brown outside and raw within, and nobody forgives that. Fold it when the top is barely set and let the heat finish the middle on its way to the table. Then carry it in with both hands, and enjoy the silence.

## 1225760 Sunrise Omelette

An omelette of giant eggs seasoned with holy salts, which someone decided was a sunrise. I suppose it is the colour: a very bright yolk, a pale white, and a pinch of salt that the clergy blessed and the merchants priced accordingly. Cook it slowly, so the colour stays bright. A sunrise cooked fast turns to noon, and nobody writes poems about noon. I serve it to priests, who appreciate the joke, and to paladins, who do not.

## 1294014 Sweetpaw Jam

Jam from the berries of Hyjal, which grow on the slopes of a mountain I am told is sacred and which I am fairly sure is very cold. Berries and soothing spice, cooked down slowly until a drop on a cold plate wrinkles when pushed. That is your test. Too runny, and you have made sauce. Too stiff, and you have made a brick. Sweetpaw, they call it, after the bears who raid the bushes. The bears are right about the berries.

## 22761 Runn Tum Tuber Surprise

The recipe for this dish is carried by an imp in the ruins of Dire Maul, who will not part with it until he has led you on a merry chase through halls full of very angry satyrs. I know this because a pupil of mine made the chase and returned with the recipe, a limp and an opinion of imps that I share. The runn tum tuber grows in the shadows of those ruins and tastes, cooked well, like the best root you never knew existed: earthy, sweet and faintly smoky. Peel it, slice it thick and roast it slowly with soothing spice until the edges caramelise and the centre gives to a fork. The surprise is that anything this good comes from a place that bad. I teach the recipe gladly. I do not teach the chase, and I have advised my pupil never to repeat it.

## 24801 Smoked Desert Dumplings

This recipe is not sold. It is taught, by the cooks of the Cenarion Circle in Silithus, to those who have proven themselves in a desert that wants them dead. I have not been to Silithus. I do not need to have been to know that sandworm meat is a challenge: dense, pale and fond of sand in every crease. Wash it until the water runs clear, chop it fine with soothing spice, and roll it into small dumplings, pressed tight so they hold together in the smoke. Then smoke them slowly over a low fire. The smoke flavours the outside and the steam cooks the middle, and when they are done they are firm, fragrant and entirely free of sand, which is the true achievement. The Circle guards this recipe jealously, and I respect them for it. I have guarded worse for less.
