## 1249957 Peace Tea

Peacebloom is the pale flower that grows along every road, and the Frostmane trolls of Dun Morogh brew it into this tea before they attack our dwarves. I find that reassuring, somehow. Peacebloom in spring water, brought just below a boil and left until it turns pale gold. Take it off the heat before the first bubble, because a boiled flower gives up its sweetness and keeps only its stalk. Properly made, it is the calmest cup in the kingdom. The trolls, clearly, have never had one.

## 1249963 Venomous Smoothie

A smoothie made with a venom sac, which the vendor assured me is safe, with the confidence of a man who had not yet drunk one. The sac is broken into spring water with mild spice and the whole of it passed through fine cloth, so the drink keeps the venom's edge and leaves the venom behind. Serve it chilled. The first mouthful is a warning and the second a pleasure, which is the right order for both. I sell nothing like it. I may yet, if the vendor survives.

## 21144 Egg Nog

Egg nog is the drink of Winter Veil and the reason so many Winter Veil mornings begin late. The egg is beaten into ice cold milk with the holiday spices until it is pale, thick and smells like the inside of a good cupboard. The holiday spirits come last. Measure them as if the guests could see you, which they can. Serve it cold, in small cups, and do not refill the cups of people who have begun to sing.

## 9513 Thistle Tea

Thistle tea is the drink of this kingdom's rogues, who brew it from swiftthistle and spring water before doing whatever it is rogues do. I do not ask. The water should have stopped boiling a breath before it meets the leaf, and the leaf should leave the cup the moment the colour comes, green with a little gold. Left longer, it grows harsh. Rogues drink it harsh. I prefer to know where my purse is while they drink it.

## 1249958 Royal Tea

Mageroyal is a herb with a high opinion of itself, and royal tea is mageroyal steeped in ice cold milk, which gives the milk a high opinion of itself as well. The milk is warmed gently and the herb left in it until the milk takes on a pale violet tint and the smell of a fine garden. Do not boil it. Boiled milk forms a skin, and a skin on royal tea is a disgrace, and a common one. I serve it to nobles, who enjoy the irony less than I do.

## 1249964 Slimy Smoothie

Slimy, it calls itself, and it is the most accurate name in this chapter. Spider ichor, mild spice and ice cold milk, shaken until the ichor stops sulking at the bottom of the glass and agrees to be a drink. Serve it at once, because it does not stay agreeable. I would not have invented this. I have, however, watched a gnome drink three, which I think says more about gnomes than about the smoothie.

## 1249965 Mrrggl Smrrthle

I will not try to pronounce the name of this drink. I have heard a murloc try, and it did not go well for either of us. The fin must be fresh and shaken hard in melon juice with hot spice, long enough that the juice turns faintly cloudy and takes on the sea. It is sweet, then hot, then something I can only call coastal. The murlocs drink it as an insult to the rest of us. It is, I admit, a well-made insult.

## 1249959 Root Tea

Liferoot, steeped in melon juice, which is an odd way to make tea and a good one. The melon juice is warmed until it just steams, and the liferoot, washed and sliced, is left in it until the juice darkens to the colour of good oak. Strain it through cloth. Leave the root in too long and the ground it grew in joins you at the table, uninvited. The murlocs of Hillsbrad carry this recipe, which I take as proof that even murlocs have afternoons.

## 13028 Goldthorn Tea

Goldthorn grows in the hard places, Stranglethorn and the Hinterlands, and it makes a tea that every trainer teaches and every soldier carries. It is strong, and it is a mistake to insult it with a furious kettle. Let the kettle rest a moment, pour, and lift the goldthorn out when the cup goes the amber of a good lamp. A goldthorn left in the cup makes the tea bitter enough to strip paint. I have seen it done. The paint did not recover.

## 1249966 Calcified Smoothie

A smoothie with a fang in it. I read the recipe twice, then a third time with my spectacles on. Sweet nectar and hot spice, with a large fang ground fine and stirred through. The fang gives the drink a chalky body and makes it a favourite of people who fight bears. Strain it well. A drink with grit in it is a drink with a complaint in it, and I do not take complaints.

## 1249960 Triage Tea

A tea for after a fight, by its name, made of sweet nectar and fadeleaf. Fadeleaf is the herb that rogues chew to go unseen, which seems to me a curious thing to give the wounded. The nectar is warmed to a steam, never a boil, and the fadeleaf comes out the moment the colour turns, because after that it gives only bitterness. No merchant has yet offered me this recipe. When one does, I shall buy it, and buy the healer who wrote it a drink.

## 1249967 Spicy Smoothie

A drink with a flame sac in it, which the fire mages consider a recommendation. Moonberry juice and soothing spice go in the glass, and the sac is pierced over it with a pin, once, at arm's length. Shake it hard and drink it fast, before the heat makes up its mind. It is the only drink I know that warms the hands from the inside. The fire mages of the Mage Quarter order it by the jug, which tells you all you need to know about fire mages.

## 1249961 Sunny Tea

Sungrass, steeped in moonberry juice, which is a joke about the sun and the moon that the recipe does not trouble to explain. The juice is warmed and the sungrass steeped until the drink turns the gold of late sun. Strain it and serve it warm, never hot. It is a cheerful drink, which is rare, and I have nothing cutting to say about it, which is rarer still.

## 1249968 Wicked Smoothie

A drink named for its worst ingredient, which is a rare piece of honesty in a recipe. The wicked claw is ground to dust, because a shard in the throat is a poor way to end an evening, and stirred into morning glory dew with a heavy hand of soothing spice. The spice is a peace offering. The claw refuses it. What you drink is bitter, dark and faintly hostile. I have never served it for pleasure. I serve it to pupils who boast, and they boast less afterwards.

## 1249962 Sage's Tea

Mountain silversage steeped in morning glory dew. The sages of Stormwind drink it while they think, which is to say all day, and charge the cost to the Crown. The dew is warmed only until it is no longer cold, and the silversage kept in only until the dew takes a faint green. It is a very good tea. It is also an expensive way to feel wise, which is the most common kind.
