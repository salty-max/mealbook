## 1249957 Peace Tea

Peacebloom is the pale flower that grows along every road, and the Frostmane trolls of Dun Morogh, I am told, brew it into this tea before they attack our dwarves. I find that reassuring, somehow. Two peacebloom flowers to a measure of spring water, brought just below a boil and left to steep until the water turns the colour of straw. Boil it, and it tastes of hay. Steep it, and it tastes of a quiet afternoon. The trolls, clearly, have never had one.

## 1249963 Venomous Smoothie

A smoothie made with a venom sac, which the vendor assured me is safe, with the confidence of a man who had not yet drunk one. Spring water, mild spice, and the small venom sac, crushed and strained so that only its sharpness remains and not its purpose. Shake it hard and drink it cold. It bites the tongue on the way down, which I gather is the point. I sell nothing like it. I may yet, if the vendor survives.

## 21144 Egg Nog

Egg nog is the drink of Winter Veil and the reason so many Winter Veil mornings begin late. An egg, ice cold milk, holiday spirits and holiday spices, beaten together until it is pale and thick and smells like the inside of a good cupboard. The spirits go in last and in moderation, which no one has ever achieved. Serve it cold, in small cups, and do not refill the cups of people who have begun to sing.

## 9513 Thistle Tea

Thistle tea is the drink of this kingdom's rogues, who brew it from swiftthistle and spring water and drink it before doing whatever it is rogues do. I do not ask. Steep the swiftthistle in water just off the boil until it turns green-gold, then strain it. Swiftthistle steeped too long turns bitter, which, I am told, rogues prefer. I prefer to know where my purse is when they drink it.

## 1249958 Royal Tea

Mageroyal is a herb with a high opinion of itself, and royal tea is mageroyal steeped in ice cold milk, which gives the milk a high opinion of itself as well. The milk is warmed gently and the herb left in it until the milk takes on a pale violet tint and the smell of a fine garden. Do not boil it. Boiled milk forms a skin, and a skin on royal tea is a peasant's crown. I serve it to nobles, who enjoy the irony less than I do.

## 1249964 Slimy Smoothie

Slimy, it calls itself, and it is the most accurate name in this chapter. Ice cold milk, mild spice and spider ichor, shaken until the ichor stops sulking at the bottom of the glass and agrees to be a drink. Serve it at once, because it does not stay agreeable. I would not have invented this. I have, however, watched a gnome drink three, which I think says more about gnomes than about the smoothie.

## 1249965 Mrrggl Smrrthle

I will not try to pronounce the name of this drink. I have heard a murloc try, and it did not go well for either of us. Melon juice, hot spice and a murloc fin, shaken together until the fin gives up its body to the juice. It is sweet, then hot, then something I can only call coastal. The murlocs, I suspect, drink it as an insult to the rest of us. It is, I admit, a well-made insult.

## 1249959 Root Tea

Liferoot, steeped in melon juice, which is an odd way to make tea and a good one. The melon juice is warmed until it just steams, and the liferoot, washed and sliced, left in it until the juice goes dark and smells of earth and summer at once. Strain it carefully. A root left in too long makes the tea taste of the ground it grew in, and the ground was not invited. The murlocs of Hillsbrad carry this recipe, which I take as proof that even murlocs have afternoons.

## 13028 Goldthorn Tea

Goldthorn grows in the hard places, Stranglethorn and the Hinterlands, and it makes a tea that every trainer teaches and every soldier carries. One goldthorn makes four cups, which tells you how strong it is. Steep it in water just off the boil until the water turns the colour of old brass, then strain it hard. A goldthorn left in the cup makes the tea bitter enough to strip paint. I have seen it done. The paint did not recover.

## 1249966 Calcified Smoothie

A smoothie with a fang in it. I read the recipe twice, then a third time with my spectacles on. Sweet nectar and hot spice, and a large fang ground to a fine powder and stirred in, which gives the drink a chalky body and, I am told, makes it a favourite of people who fight bears. Strain it well. A drink with grit in it is a drink with a complaint in it, and I do not take complaints.

## 1249960 Triage Tea

A tea for after a fight, I gather from the name, made of sweet nectar and fadeleaf. Fadeleaf is the herb that rogues chew to go unseen, which seems to me a curious thing to give the wounded. Warm the nectar until it steams, steep the fadeleaf in it and strain it before it turns bitter. I have not yet seen this recipe sold. When I do, I shall buy it, and buy the healer who wrote it a drink.

## 1249967 Spicy Smoothie

Moonberry juice, soothing spice and a small flame sac, pierced and squeezed into the glass. The soothing spice is there, I suspect, to apologise for the flame sac. Shake it hard and drink it fast, before the heat makes up its mind. It is the only drink I know that warms the hands from the inside. The fire mages of the Mage Quarter order it by the jug, which tells you all you need to know about fire mages.

## 1249961 Sunny Tea

Sungrass, steeped in moonberry juice, which is a joke about the sun and the moon that the recipe does not trouble to explain. The juice is warmed and the sungrass left in it until the drink turns gold and smells of hay in the afternoon. Strain it and serve it warm. It is a cheerful drink, which is rare, and I have nothing cutting to say about it, which is rarer still.

## 1249968 Wicked Smoothie

Morning glory dew, a great deal of soothing spice and a wicked claw, ground fine. Wicked is the right word. The claw gives the drink a dark, bitter edge that the spice spends its whole life trying to smooth over, and never quite does. Shake it cold. I do not know who drinks this for pleasure. I know who drinks it on a dare, and I have shown several of them out of my kitchen.

## 1249962 Sage's Tea

Mountain silversage steeped in morning glory dew, which is a tea made entirely of things that are difficult to find. That, I suspect, is why it is called a sage's tea. Warm the dew gently, steep the silversage until the drink is pale and smells of cold air and stone, then strain it. It is a very good tea. It is also an expensive way to feel wise, which is the most common kind.
