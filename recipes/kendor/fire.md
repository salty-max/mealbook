## 1229737 Basic Campfire

Before there is a dish, there is a fire, and before there is a fire there is a cook who knows how to build one. Dry wood, laid crosswise with room to breathe, and the flame put to the bottom of the pile, never the top. Then leave it alone. A cook who prods a young fire has not learned to wait. A cook who makes a roaring one is a show-off. The right fire for cooking is the one that has stopped trying to impress you and settled down to coals.

## 1283400 Journeyman Campfire

Star wood burns hotter and cleaner than the common kind, and a journeyman cook should know the difference before he is allowed near a better fire. The recipe was found in the Wailing Caverns, among the druids who went wrong there, which is a long way to go for a better campfire. Stack it as loosely as the basic fire, and wait longer for the coals. A hotter fire forgives less. That is what it means to be a journeyman.

## 1291341 Expert Campfire

Thick logs, which take an age to catch and then burn for an evening, steady as a hearth. This is the fire of an expert, and it teaches an expert's lesson: the long, even heat is worth the wait. Lay the logs in a square, leave a gap for the air, light the kindling beneath and be patient. I have not yet seen this recipe sold. I have seen cooks who thought they did not need it, and they were never quite experts.

## 1263067 Iron Oven

An iron oven, says the recipe, and then asks for flour, spring water and soothing spice, and no iron at all. I have read it several times. I believe the oven is the vessel and the recipe is what goes into it first. A great quantity of dough is worked with spice and baked to season the iron, as one seasons a new pan. Whatever its secret, it is a master's recipe, and I do not yet possess it, which annoys me more than I can politely say.
