## 1229737 Basic Campfire

Before there is a dish, there is a fire, and before there is a fire there is a cook who knows how to build one. Simple wood, stacked loosely so the air can move through it, lit at the bottom and left alone. A cook who cannot make a fire is a guest. A cook who makes a roaring one is a show-off. The right fire for cooking is the one that has stopped trying to impress you and settled down to coals.

## 1283400 Journeyman Campfire

Star wood burns hotter and cleaner than the common kind, and a journeyman cook should know the difference before he is allowed near a better fire. The recipe was found in the Wailing Caverns, among the druids who went wrong there, which is a long way to go for a better campfire. Stack it the same way, loosely, and let it burn down further before you cook. A hotter fire forgives less. That is what it means to be a journeyman.

## 1291341 Expert Campfire

Thick logs, which take an age to catch and then burn for an evening, steady as a hearth. This is the fire of an expert, and it teaches an expert's lesson: the long, even heat is worth the wait. Lay the logs in a square, leave a gap for the air, light the kindling beneath and be patient. I have not yet seen this recipe sold. I have seen cooks who thought they did not need it, and they were never quite experts.

## 1263067 Iron Oven

An iron oven, says the recipe, and then asks for flour, spring water and soothing spice, and no iron at all. I have read it several times. I believe the oven is the vessel and the recipe is what goes into it first, a great quantity of dough worked with spice and baked to season the iron, as a new pan is seasoned. Whatever its secret, it is a master's recipe, and I do not yet possess it, which annoys me more than I can politely say.
