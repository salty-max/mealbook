## 7751 Brilliant Smallfish

The first fish of every child with a pole, and the first fish many cooks never get past. A smallfish is a mouthful, and a mouthful forgives nothing. Cook it whole, in a hot pan, and only until the eye clouds over. That is your clock, and the fish carries it for you. The fishermen at the harbour sell these by the bucket to children, who burn them. I do not sell them. I correct them.

## 7752 Slitherskin Mackerel

Mackerel is an oily fish, and oil is a gift that most cooks treat as a problem. They drown it, they scrape it, they apologise for it. Lay it skin down on a dry, hot pan and let its own oil cook it. The skin crisps, the flesh stays sweet, and the kitchen smells of the sea for a day, which is no punishment. I learned this on the docks as a boy. I have improved it only slightly, which for me is a compliment.

## 1247741 Spinefin Halibut

A fish I have not yet seen in Stormwind's market, though the fishermen speak of it the way they speak of every fish they have not caught. Halibut is a broad, white, generous fish, and a spined one, I am told, fights back on the line and in the kitchen. Cook it in a thick piece, gently, until the flakes just part under a finger. Halibut overcooked is chalk. When someone brings me one, I will say more, and I will say it first.

## 1270635 Pincer Bites

Crab season, they call the errand that teaches this, as though crabs had seasons and were not simply always there, scuttling at the edge of every beach. The claws are cracked, the meat picked out and bound with a little flour, then fried in small bites. Use the least flour you can manage. It holds the crab together. It must not become the dish. A bite that tastes of bread is a bite that lied to you about the beach.

## 6499 Boiled Clams

A clam is a mouthful of the sea, and the only way to ruin it is to forget it in the pot, which is what everyone does. The sailors at the harbour boil them in seawater and call it tradition. I call it laziness with a view. Use good spring water, count to sixty slowly, and take them out. Rubber is for boots. If you feel the need for a sauce, you overcooked them.

## 6415 Fillet of Frenzy

The frenzies of Darkshore are all teeth and temper, and the night elves fillet them with a reverence I find excessive. Alegorn would have you lay the fillet on a leaf and murmur over it. I would have you season it lightly and cook it fast, because frenzy flesh is soft and falls apart if you so much as look at it slowly. Skin side down, a hot pan, one turn. The leaf is optional. The murmuring is not my business.

## 7754 Loch Frenzy Delight

The dwarves of Loch Modan named this dish a delight, which is the most generous word I have ever heard a dwarf use about anything without malt in it. The frenzy of the loch is firmer than its southern cousins, cold-water flesh that holds together in the pan. Season it with mild spice, cook it quickly and serve it hot. I will grant the dwarves the name. I will not grant them the method, which involves a great deal of shouting.

## 7753 Longjaw Mud Snapper

A snapper from the muddy bottoms of slow rivers, and it tastes of them unless you know better. I know better. Clean it well, cook it whole over a moderate fire, and lift the skin away before serving. The mud is in the skin, not the flesh. Fishermen eat it skin and all and tell you the river is part of the charm. The river is part of the river. Leave it there.

## 7827 Rainbow Fin Albacore

Albacore is a fish for people who think fish should behave like meat. It is firm, dark and rich, and it takes a hard sear on the outside and very little cooking in the middle. The sailors of Menethil cook it through until it is grey and blame the fish. I sell no recipe for it, but I would, and I would charge more. Pink in the middle is correct. Grey is a confession.

## 2544 Crab Cake

Every trainer teaches crab cakes, and every trainer teaches them too wet. Crawler meat, picked clean, a pinch of mild spice, pressed together with your hands and nothing else, then fried until the outside sets into a crust that holds the rest. If it falls apart, you pressed too little. If it bounces, you pressed too much. A crab cake should be firm the way a handshake is firm, and no firmer.

## 25704 Smoked Sagefish

Sagefish come from the lakes of Hillsbrad, where the water is cold and clear and the fishermen are patient. The fish takes on the smell of the sage along the banks, which is where its name comes from, and smoking it gently brings that smell forward. Low smoke, a pinch of spice, and an afternoon. The farmers of Southshore sell it from their windows. It is the best thing in Southshore, which is a low bar, nobly cleared.

## 2545 Cooked Crab Claw

The Westfall coast is crawling with crabs, and the Defias smugglers who hide there have, I am told, stolen my recipe for their claws. I am flattered. A claw should be cracked before it meets the fire, so the heat reaches the meat inside rather than steaming it in its own armour. A pinch of mild spice in the crack. A short, hot fire. Then pick it out with a fork, and remember that even thieves eat well when they steal from the right man.

## 8238 Savory Deviate Delight

Deviate fish come from the poisoned pools of the Wailing Caverns, in the Barrens, and I have never had the slightest wish to fetch one. The recipe is found in the bellies of the kodos that wander there, which tells you how the kodos feel about it. Cook it through, with mild spice, and eat it at your own risk. I am told it has peculiar consequences for those who eat it. I am told a great many things about the Barrens.

## 1252566 Savory Whimsyfin Delight

Whimsyfin. Somebody named a fish that, and somebody else agreed. The recipe turns up in Redridge in the packs of gnolls and murlocs, who are not known for their kitchens, so I assume they stole it from someone who was. The fish is small and bright and delicate. A pinch of mild spice, a hot pan, very little time. The name promises whimsy. The dish delivers supper, which is better.

## 7755 Bristle Whisker Catfish

Catfish is a river fish, wide-mouthed and whiskered and generally despised by people who have never cooked it properly. Skin it, always. The skin of a catfish tastes of the riverbed, and the riverbed is not a flavour. The flesh beneath is white and sweet and takes well to a hot pan and a quick turn. The fishermen of the Hillsbrad rivers swear by it. For once, fishermen and I agree.

## 6500 Goblin Deviled Clams

The goblins devil their clams the way they do business: with too much heat and a great deal of noise. I teach the recipe anyway, with the heat corrected. Tangy clam meat, hot spice, and a fire that is hot but not furious. A clam cooked furiously turns to leather. A clam cooked well has just enough fight left to remind you it came from the sea. The goblins would charge you double for the leather.

## 7828 Rockscale Cod

Cod is the fish of cold seas and honest kitchens, and rockscale cod is the best of them, so called because its scales would blunt a cheap knife. Scale it before you do anything else, with a good blade and some patience. Then a hot pan and a single turn. The flesh is white and comes apart in great clean flakes, and it needs nothing, not even an opinion. I give it one anyway. Excellent.

## 7213 Giant Clam Scorcho

Booty Bay's cooks scorch a giant clam in hot spice and serve it to pirates, who would eat anything that did not bite back and several things that did. I have refined it. Giant clam meat is dense and sweet, and the trick is to slice it thin before the heat touches it, so the spice reaches through. Then fire, fiercely and quickly. A scorcho should scorch the cook's eyebrows and spare the clam.

## 20916 Mithril Headed Trout

A trout with a silvery head the colour of mithril, which explains the name, and a temper to match, which explains the fishermen's bandages. It is a mountain fish and cold-blooded in every sense. Cook it whole, skin on, in a hot pan, and baste it with its own fat. The flesh is pale and fine. The head stays on for the table. A trout without its head looks embarrassed, and so does the cook who served it.

## 25954 Sagefish Delight

The greater sagefish is the sagefish grown up, with more of everything, including the smell of the herbs along its lake. Hot spice brings it forward without burying it. Sear the fillet skin side down until the skin crisps, then turn it for a moment, no more. Delight is a strong word for a fish. I have tasted this one, and I am not withdrawing it.

## 1319313 Plated Armorfish

A fish that wears armour, which seems only fair, given what happens to the ones that do not. The plates come off with a heavy knife and some patience, and the flesh beneath is firm and pale, like a lobster's tail. Soothing spice, a moderate heat, and do not hurry it. Armoured fish take their time to cook and their time to leave the shell. So should you.

## 18239 Cooked Glossy Mightfish

A big, glossy fish from the warm waters of the south, rich enough to be served in thick steaks. Soothing spice suits it, rounding the richness rather than fighting it. Sear the steaks hard and let them rest, as you would meat. The sailors of Booty Bay cook it in great slabs over driftwood and eat it with their knives. I have seen it. I still see it, some nights.

## 18241 Filet of Redgill

Redgill is a coastal fish, named for the flush along its gills, which fades once it is caught and cooked, as most things do. It needs nothing. A fillet, a hot pan, the skin side down until it lifts on its own. I have watched cooks reach for every spice on the shelf with this fish, out of nerves. A good redgill is not a problem to be solved. It is a fish to be left alone.

## 15915 Spiced Chili Crab

Chili crab is a dish of the hot south, where cooks believe spice is a form of hospitality. Tender crab meat, a great deal of hot spice, and a pan hot enough to make you step back. The crab must be cooked in the spice, not under it, so stir it constantly. It is messy to eat and impossible to eat politely. I serve it with a warning and a large napkin, and I keep the napkin.

## 18238 Spotted Yellowtail

Yellowtail from the warm waters off Tanaris, sold by a goblin who weighs his fish with one hand and the customer with the other. The fish is excellent anyway. It is firm and oily and needs only a hot pan and a single turn. Do not fuss with it. A spotted yellowtail is the rare fish that is better the less a cook does, and many cooks find that unbearable.

## 18240 Grilled Squid

Squid is cooked either for a moment or for an hour, and anything between is rubber. I prefer the moment. Score the body in a crosshatch, rub in a little soothing spice, and lay it on the hottest grill you own for as long as it takes to say its name twice. It curls, it chars at the edges, it is done. The cooks of the south give it an hour in a stew. They are wrong, but in an interesting way.

## 18242 Hot Smoked Bass

Hot smoking is smoking at a heat that also cooks, which is faster than cold smoking and less forgiving. Rub the summer bass with hot spice, hang it close above a smoky fire, and watch it. When the flesh turns from glass to white, it is done. Bass smoked this way keeps for days and travels well. It is the only fish I would trust to a soldier's pack, and I have trusted it to several.

## 18244 Poached Sunscale Salmon

Poaching is the art of cooking in water that never quite boils, and it is the most underrated art in any kitchen. The sunscale salmon, from the warm seas, is rich and soft and would fall apart under a harsher method. Bring the water to a shiver, not a boil, lower the fish in and leave it. When it turns opaque, lift it out. The water should never once have raised its voice.

## 1250158 Clam Linguine

Linguine, I am told, is a long flat noodle from some distant kitchen. There is no noodle in the recipe. There is zesty clam meat, a great deal of it, sliced into long ribbons and tossed in soothing spice, which I suppose is where the name came from. Cook the ribbons in a hot pan for a moment and serve them at once. They tangle like noodles and eat like clams. I approve of the deception.

## 18247 Baked Salmon

Whitescale salmon from the cold rivers, baked whole with soothing spice in a moderate oven. A whole fish bakes evenly where a fillet dries at the edges, and the skin protects the flesh like a lid. When the eye turns white and the fin pulls away with no effort, it is done. Feralas sells this recipe to the few who come that far. They deserve it, for the journey alone.

## 18246 Mightfish Steak

A mightfish is large enough to be cut into steaks like a bull, and it should be treated like one. Hot spice and soothing spice, one to wake the meat and one to calm it. Sear the steaks hard on both sides and rest them before serving. The fishermen of the far south bring these in with nets and a great deal of shouting. I prefer them on a plate, in silence, with the applause afterwards.

## 470370 Smoked Redgill

A recipe sold in Silithus, by the expedition's quartermasters, to soldiers who needed food that would keep in the sand. Redgill, smoked slowly until it is dry enough to travel and still tender enough to eat. No spice, only smoke. The soldiers who brought it back to Stormwind asked me to approve of it. I did, which surprised them more than the desert had.
