## 2538 Charred Wolf Meat

Every cook begins here, with a fire, a knife and a wolf that did not want to be dinner. Most of them never move on, which is how the roads of this kingdom came to smell of burnt wolf. Charring is not burning. You want the outside dark and crisp and the inside still yielding, and that means a fire that has settled to coals, not one still showing off its flames. Learn this, and you have learned patience. Then come and see me.

## 15935 Crispy Bat Wing

This recipe comes from Tirisfal, where the cooks are dead and the bats are not, yet. It reached me through a merchant I trust more than his goods. I will grant it this: a wing is mostly skin, and skin rewards a cook who leaves it alone. Lay it on a hot iron, skin down, and do not touch it until it lets go of the pan on its own. That is the whole art. The rest of Tirisfal's cooking I leave to Tirisfal.

## 2540 Roasted Boar Meat

Elwynn's boars have fed this city since before it had walls, and they will feed it after the walls need rebuilding again. Tomas in Goldshire roasts them whole for his inn, turning the spit by hand and calling it tradition. It is a fine tradition. It is also why his boar is done on one side and hopeful on the other. Cut the meat into even pieces, so the heat treats them all alike. Fairness, in a kitchen, is a matter of knife work.

## 6412 Kaldorei Spider Kabob

The night elves of Teldrassil will teach you this kabob for the price of a few spider legs, which tells you what they think of spiders. Alegorn in Darnassus would have you thread each leg with a sprig of something green and arrange the skewers like a poem. Ignore the poem. Spider legs want a fierce, short heat, enough to crack the shell and no more, or the meat inside turns to string. Then you may garnish, if you must. I never must.

## 2539 Spiced Wolf Meat

The second dish every trainer teaches, and the first one most cooks get wrong. They believe spice is a disguise. They take a wolf that has run itself lean in the hills and bury it, and the wolf deserved better. Rub the spice in before the meat meets the fire, then leave it to settle. Spice added at the end sits on the meat like a hat. Spice added at the start becomes part of it.

## 6413 Scorpid Surprise

A dish of the Horde, from the dust of Durotar, and the surprise is that anyone eats a scorpid on purpose. I have no need to taste the cooking of Orgrimmar. I have, however, read the recipe, because a master reads everything. Its one wisdom is the stinger. Cooked until it turns from glass to chalk, it loses its venom and keeps its bite. Undercooked, it keeps both. I leave you to decide which of those you would call a surprise.

## 1250156 Tasty Raptor Bites

A recipe that turns up in the nests of raptors, which is either a joke or a warning. The bites are made from the egg, not the beast, and a raptor egg is a heavier, richer thing than any hen ever laid. Season it more generously than you think decent, because the yolk drinks every grain. Cook the bites small and quick. A raptor egg cooked slowly turns to rubber out of spite. I suspect the mother taught it that.

## 2795 Beer Basted Boar Ribs

Ragnar Thunderbrew believes this recipe belongs to his family. I sell it in Stormwind at a fair price, and I have improved it. He will tell you the ribs need Rhapsody Malt from the first minute to the last. They do not. The dwarves baste from the start and then wonder why the outside burns while the inside sulks. Roast the rib bare until the fat runs clear, and only then begin the malt, a little at a time, so it turns to a glaze instead of a char. The result is sweet, dark and sticky, and it is the finest thing to come out of Dun Morogh. I would tell Ragnar so, if he could hear anything over his own tavern. I suspect he knows. His ribs have improved since.

## 6414 Roasted Kodo Meat

Kodo is the meat of Mulgore, roasted there by tauren who cook under open sky with the plains for a kitchen. I have never stood under that sky, and I have no plans to. A kodo is an enormous, patient animal, and its meat is patient too: tough at first, generous at the end. Roast it slowly and rest it as long as it roasted. The tauren are right about one thing, and it is this: some meat cannot be hurried by anyone.

## 8607 Smoked Bear Meat

Smoking is the dwarves' art, and I say so without pleasure. In Loch Modan they hang bear meat in the smoke of their chimneys and forget it there through the winter, and by spring it is better than anything they meant to cook. The secret is distance. The meat hangs high, where the heat never reaches and only the smoke does the work. Close to the fire, you are roasting badly. Far from it, you are smoking well.

## 2541 Coyote Steak

The coyotes of Westfall eat whatever the farmers left behind, and the meat tastes of a hard life. You cannot change its past. You can respect it. Cut the steak thick, sear it hard, and take it off the heat while the middle still blushes. A thin coyote steak cooked through is a sandal. A thick one, rested, is supper. Every apprentice arrives thinking a steak is a simple thing. It is. That is why it is so easy to ruin.

## 3371 Blood Sausage

Thelsamar's dwarves make blood sausage every winter and send a few to Ironforge, where Daryl Riknussun praises them loudly and then teaches his students to make them worse. I sell the recipe here, corrected. Bear meat, cleaned intestines and spider ichor, which binds as blood once did and keeps far better. Stuff the casing loosely. A tight sausage splits in the pan and empties itself onto the coals, and no one has ever loved a sausage that gave up.

## 1250154 Twice-Spiced Raptor Slice

Twice spiced, and for once a dish tells the truth about itself. The raptor is rubbed once before it is sliced and once after it is cooked, and the second time is not for flavour but for the crust. Raptor flesh is lean and quick to dry. Slice it across the grain, thin enough to see a candle through, and give it the fire for a breath, no more. A recipe I did not invent, and would have, given time.

## 3370 Crocolisk Steak

Crocolisk is the steak for those who think beef too obliging. The Wetlands are full of them, and the hunters of Menethil bring me the tails in barrels of brine. The meat is pale and firm, closer to fish than to beast. Cook it as you would a thick fish: hot, short, and never twice. Reheated crocolisk tastes of the swamp it came from, which is a taste no one has ever asked for.

## 2546 Dry Pork Ribs

Dry ribs are a farmer's dish, a boar's ribs and a rub of spice with no sauce at all. Some cooks are frightened of that. They believe a rib must drip to be good. A rib must be cooked to be good. Rub the spice in the night before, let the fire die down to a low glow, and give the ribs the whole afternoon. They come out dark, crisp and dry to the touch, and they leave nothing on the bone.

## 6418 Crispy Lizard Tail

A Barrens dish, sold at the Crossroads and eaten by the Horde with their fingers. I mention the fingers only so that you do not imitate them. A thunder lizard's tail is all muscle, and the crisp comes from cooking it in its own fat until the skin shatters. Hot spice, a hot pan, and a cook brave enough to leave it there. I will not pretend to admire the Barrens. I will admit the tail is well conceived.

## 2549 Seasoned Wolf Kabob

This is the dish I would put my name to, and I have. Lean wolf flank and Stormwind Seasoning Herbs, nothing else, and anyone who offers you a substitute for the herbs is selling something. Leave a gap between the pieces on the skewer, so the heat reaches every side. Rest it before you eat it. A kabob rushed off the fire weeps on the plate, and so will you.

## 2548 Succulent Pork Ribs

The gnolls of Redridge carry this recipe around in their packs, stolen from some farmhouse, and do nothing with it. I sell the same recipe in Stormwind and do a great deal with it. Boar ribs and hot spice, and the whole secret is the turning. Turn them every few minutes, so the fat bastes the meat as it renders. Succulent is not a promise in the name. It is a reward for attention.

## 3397 Big Bear Steak

The bears of Ashenvale grow larger than ours, on a diet of night elf patience and whatever they find under the trees. Their meat is dark and rich and wants a hot spice to cut it. Sear it hard and let it rest a long while, longer than a smaller animal, because a big steak keeps cooking after it leaves the fire. In Desolace, I hear, a goblin machine sells my recipes one copy at a time. I would like to meet its cook.

## 6419 Lean Venison

Venison is the meat of a cook with good manners. A stag is lean and proud and must be treated as such, with plenty of spice rubbed in and very little time on the fire. Overcook a stag and you have insulted the forest. The elves of Ashenvale sell this recipe, which I find touching, since they spend so much of their lives keeping anyone from hunting their stags. Cook it rare, slice it thin, and say nothing about where it came from.

## 15853 Lean Wolf Steak

A plain steak from the flank of a wolf, and plainness is the hardest thing to cook. There is no sauce to hide behind and no stew to bury a mistake in. The flank is lean, so it must be seared fast and rested long, with a little spice to remind it that it is food. Rare is correct. Medium is forgivable. Beyond that, I suggest a different trade, and I know several that need no fire at all.

## 3398 Hot Lion Chops

Lions of the Barrens, cut into chops and burned with hot spice by cooks who call the result a delicacy. I call it a lion that deserved better. A lion's meat is lean and sweet close to the bone, and the chop should be cooked on the bone for that reason. Hot spice is welcome. Hot fire is not. Give the chops a moderate heat and turn them often. A lion should arrive at the table looking as if it lost a fair fight.

## 3399 Tasty Lion Steak

Tasty, it calls itself, and it is too modest by half. Lion and soothing spice, cooked as a steak rather than chopped, which is the only civilised way to eat a lion. The soothing spice is not there to calm the cook. It rounds the meat's sweetness. Sear it, rest it, slice it across the grain. A lion steak sliced along the grain chews like an argument you cannot win.

## 1250151 Bear Brisket

Brisket is the cut of a patient cook, and bear brisket is the cut of a saint. It is the toughest part of the animal, all hard work and old muscle. Rub it with hot spice and give it the lowest fire you can keep alight and the whole of a day. Somewhere in the evening it gives up and falls apart under a fork. I cannot tell you the hour. I can tell you that the cook who checks it too often never reaches it.

## 4094 Barbecued Buzzard Wing

In the Badlands they will tell you a buzzard is not worth the coals. They have not eaten it barbecued. A buzzard wing is mostly sinew and spite, so it wants hot spice and a long, low heat, then a short, fierce one at the end to blister the skin. The hunters of Arathi sell the recipe for a pittance, which is a crime against cooking, and I have reported it to no one.

## 15863 Carrion Surprise

Carrion Surprise. I have read the recipe twice, in case the name was a joke. It is not. Mystery meat and hot spice, cooked into a dish that the trolls of the Swamp of Sorrows sell as though it were a kindness. The meat must be cooked through and then cooked through again, because whatever it once was, it has been dead a while. I do not serve this. I teach it, so that my pupils know what they are avoiding.

## 15856 Hot Wolf Ribs

The wolves of the far south are red, ill-tempered and larger than ours, and their ribs come with a great deal of meat attached. Feralas sells this recipe to anyone passing through, which few do. Hot spice, a hot fire, and then patience, because red wolf is fattier than our grey kind and needs time to render. When the fat runs clear and the bones turn dark, they are done. When the fat smokes, so is your reputation.

## 15855 Roast Raptor

Roast raptor is soldiers' food, sold at the camps in Stranglethorn where men eat what tried to eat them. That is reason enough to respect it. Raptor flesh is lean and quick, so it roasts best in large pieces that protect their own middles. Hot spice on the outside, a strong fire, and then away from the heat to finish. A raptor roasted like a chicken comes out like a boot. A raptor roasted like a raptor comes out like a victory.

## 1250150 Raging Raptor Ribs

A recipe I have heard described but never seen sold, which is the most dangerous kind. The raptor is long past raging by the time it reaches the pan, so the name must mean the spice, and there is a great deal of it. Cook the ribs low until the meat pulls from the bone, then over a fierce heat to set the crust. Serve them only to people who asked. I do not say this of every dish.

## 1295834 Plain Ol' Paletusk

A recipe from the Riverglades, sold by a woman who saw no reason to give it a grander name, and she was right. Boar meat, a good deal of it, with a pinch of mild spice, roasted on the bone until the fat crackles. That is all. I have spent my life teaching cooks to resist doing more, and here is a farmer who never needed teaching. Rest it before you carve it. Plain food forgives nothing, which is why it is so seldom done well.

## 21175 Spider Sausage

White spider meat, pressed into a sausage and taught by every cooking trainer in the land as though it were ordinary. It is not ordinary. Spider meat is sweet and delicate, closer to crab than to pork, and it takes no spice because it needs none. The art is the casing: firm enough to hold, thin enough to crack in the pan. Many pupils pale at the word spider. I ask them whether they have ever eaten a crab, and wait.

## 1250153 Raptor Rouladen

Rouladen is a dwarvish word, or a dwarvish claim on a word, and the dish is rolled meat: thin slices of raptor rolled around hot spice and tied. Rolled meat cooks from the outside in, so the outer turn must be thin or the middle stays raw. Brown the rolls on every side, then lower the heat and leave them be. When the string chars, cut it away. If you serve a guest the string, you deserve the look you get.

## 1250142 Savory Stag Sliders

Sliders, I am told, are small and many, which is how young cooks like everything. The stag is chopped fine with hot spice and pressed into rounds the size of a palm. Press them once and never again: a slider pressed twice gives its juice to the pan instead of the guest. I do not approve of food designed to be eaten standing. I approve of these, standing, and I am not proud of it.

## 22480 Tender Wolf Steak

Tender wolf meat comes from the wolves of Winterspring and the far cold places, where an animal lives slowly and keeps its fat. It is the best wolf meat in the world, and it is wasted on most cooks. Soothing spice, a short hot sear, and then rest it as long as you cooked it. A cook who has ruined a hundred stringy wolves in Elwynn will finally understand, on this one, what they were reaching for.

## 1225759 Filet o' Flank

Filet o' Flank, a name with a letter missing and a dish with nothing missing at all. The red wolf flank is rubbed with holy salts, which the clergy will tell you are blessed and the merchants will tell you are expensive. They are both right. The salt draws the water from the meat and firms it, so cook it quickly. A blessed steak overcooked is still overcooked. The Light will not save it, and neither will I.

## 1225758 Prowler Steak

Prowler steak is tiger, which the name is too polite to say. The steaks are cut thick, rubbed with holy salts and left an hour before the fire touches them. Tiger is a lean, hard-working meat, and the salt tames it. The rest is restraint: a hot iron, a short sear and a long rest. I have heard hunters argue that a tiger should be cooked angry. Hunters should hunt, and leave the kitchen to those who can count to sixty.

## 1250144 Bat Hachee

Hachee is an old farmhouse word for a slow stew of chopped meat, and here the meat is bat. I would not have chosen bat. Bat would not have chosen me. Wings, chopped, with soothing spice, and a low heat for most of an evening, until the sinew softens into something you could serve a guest. Whoever invented it was poor and clever. I respect the clever, and I have learned, over the years, to forgive the poor.

## 1250147 Bear Bruscitti

Bruscitti. I have looked for the word in three languages and found it in none. The dish is plain enough to explain itself: big bear meat, sliced thin, layered with soothing spice and roasted slowly until the layers fuse. The trick is to slice it cold, because warm meat tears under the knife. Whoever named this dish was trying to sound foreign. The dish itself has nothing to hide, and I like it better than its name.

## 1250152 Prehistoric Pulled Raptor

Prehistoric, as though the raptor had been waiting since the world began to be pulled apart with two forks. Perhaps it had. Raptor and a great deal of soothing spice, then the lowest fire you can tend for most of a day. The meat is done when it falls apart at a touch. Pull it with forks, never a knife. A knife makes it sliced raptor, and sliced raptor is a different and lesser book.

## 1250143 Soaring Pamplona

A pamplona is meat flattened, filled and rolled, and the soaring one is made of buzzard, which is the only thing about it that soars. The wings, boned and beaten flat, rolled around soothing spice and tied. It is fiddly work, and fiddly work is where a cook is tested. Roll it tight, roast it gently, and slice it into rounds. The rounds show your knife work. Make sure they show something you would sign.

## 1250148 Steaming Stag Steak

A stag steak served steaming, which is to say served at once, which is how every steak ought to be served and how almost none are. Stag, soothing spice rubbed in deep, a very hot iron. The steam is the point. If it reaches the table without steam, it waited somewhere it should not have, and someone in your kitchen owes you an apology, and very likely a new steak.

## 1250149 Swiftstrike Steak

A hunter's name for a cook's dish, as though the tiger had been caught by a thunderbolt. The tiger was caught by a hunter and a great deal of waiting. The steak, however, earns its name: tiger, soothing spice, and the hottest fire you can make, for the shortest time you can bear. Strike fast, rest long. The second half is where most cooks lose their nerve.

## 1250146 Flank au Poivre

Au poivre means with pepper, in some foreign kitchen, and there is no pepper in it. There is red wolf flank and soothing spice, and the spice does the pepper's work well enough that no one complains. Sear the flank hard, then let it rest while the pan cools, and pour the juices back over it. Never carve it on the board where it seared. The board remembers the heat, and so will the meat.

## 25659 Dirge's Kickin' Chimaerok Chops

Dirge Quikcleave runs a kitchen in Gadgetzan, in the middle of a desert, where he cooks chimaerok with goblin rocket fuel and charges for the privilege. I have every reason to despise this recipe. I cannot. Chimaerok tenderloin is the finest meat on Kalimdor, dense and sweet, from beasts so far away that only the brave or the hungry ever bring it back. Deeprock salt from the dwarves' mines, hot spice, and the fuel, which does not flavour the meat so much as flatter the fire into a heat no honest coal can reach. Use a great deal of distance and very little of the fuel. Dirge would tell you otherwise. Dirge has fewer eyebrows than I do. The chops come out charred and juicy and, I admit it, the best thing a goblin ever made.
