## 1255241 Cleansing Stew

A stew made with felbloom that someone has cleansed, which I am assured makes it a herb again and no longer a threat. I take the word of the druids on such matters, and on very little else. Stag meat gives it body. The giant egg goes in last, off the heat and in a thin stream, or the broth scrambles instead of turning silky. It is cooked for those coming back from Felwood, and they deserve a kinder kitchen than the forest they left.

## 6416 Strider Stew

Strider is the long-legged bird of the Barrens, all neck and opinion, and the Horde stews it with apples, which is the first sensible thing I have heard of them doing. The apple sweetens the broth and softens the meat, and the meat needs softening. Simmer it slowly until the apple has gone to nothing and the bird has given up its stubbornness. I would not cross the sea for it.

## 2543 Westfall Stew

I sell the recipe for this stew, which tells you I approve of it, the way one approves of a sturdy pair of boots. Westfall's farmers made it from whatever the land threw at them, the vulture, the snout of a goretusk, the murloc's eye, and they were right about all three. I tried it once without the eye. It was thin, and so was my pride. The eye thickens the broth. Let it simmer longer than you think it needs, then longer again. A stew rushed is a soup that lied. These days the farms stand empty and the stew is cooked by the people who lost them, in camps along the road to Sentinel Hill. It is not refined. It is honest, and in Westfall that is rarer now than refinement.

## 6501 Clam Chowder

Chowder is clams and milk, and the milk must never boil. That is the entire difficulty, and it is enough to defeat most cooks on the coast. The clam meat goes in with a little mild spice once the milk is warm, and from then on the pot stays below a simmer until it reaches the bowl. Boiled milk splits, and split chowder looks like a mistake in a bowl. The fishwives of Westfall get it right more often than the inns. The inns should be ashamed.

## 6417 Dig Rat Stew

A stew of rats, taught by the Horde in the Barrens to anyone who will catch the rats for them. I include it because I am thorough, not because I recommend it. A dig rat is small and lean and cleaner than the rats of our own sewers, which is faint praise. Stew it long. Everything about this dish suggests long cooking, including the cook's need to forget what is in the pot.

## 3372 Murloc Fin Soup

Murloc fins make a soup with more body than you would believe, if you have only met murlocs at the end of a sword. Simmer the fins with hot spice until they dissolve into the broth and give it a richness like the best bone stock. The murlocs would object, if anyone asked them. The murlocs object to everything, at length, and none of it in a language I care to learn.

## 2547 Redridge Goulash

Redridge goulash is spider and condor, stewed together by the people of Lakeshire, who have had spiders and condors to spare since the gnolls took the rest. The two meats cook at very different speeds, and the art is in the timing. The condor needs most of the afternoon to soften. The spider needs a fraction of it and goes stringy if it stays longer. Add it late. I sell the recipe with that warning written in the margin, in my own hand.

## 3373 Crocolisk Gumbo

Gumbo is a dish of the swamps, a thick, dark stew that the people of the Wetlands cook as though their lives depended on it, which in the Wetlands they sometimes do. Tender crocolisk meat and hot spice go into the pot and cook down slowly, until the gumbo coats a spoon and stays there. The colour is the measure. Too pale, and it was hurried. Too dark, and you have burnt the bottom of the pot and should start again with dignity.

## 24418 Heavy Crocolisk Stew

Heavy, it says, and it is no lie: crocolisk in quantity, with soothing spice, stewed until it is closer to a pudding than a soup. Theramore's garrison lives on it through the Dustwallow winters and swears it is the only warm thing in the marsh. They are right. The danger of a stew this dense is that the heat gathers at the base of the pot, so keep a wooden spoon moving there. Most ruined stews die down there.

## 15861 Jungle Stew

A stew from Stranglethorn, made by soldiers who had tiger meat, spring water and apples, and the wit to put them together. The apples sweeten what would otherwise be a hard, gamey broth. Brown the tiger hard first, because a stew begun pale stays pale. The water and the apples follow, and it simmers until the fruit has disappeared. A jungle produces a great deal of food and very few recipes. This is one of the good ones.

## 15865 Mystery Stew

Mystery Stew is mystery meat stewed in dwarven stout, and the mystery is which of the two does more of the work. I will tell you: the stout. A good dwarven stout stains the meat dark, loosens it, and leaves the broth a bitterness that hides a multitude of sins. Daryl Riknussun teaches this in Ironforge as a dwarven dish, which is generous of him, since the dwarves contributed only the beer and the questions. Cook it long. Whatever the meat once was, it must forget. Skim the scum as it rises. A boil sours the stout and turns the meat to string. Served in Theramore and Desolace to people who have stopped asking questions, it is, I admit, very good. I have stopped asking questions too. It was easier than I expected.

## 3400 Soothing Turtle Bisque

Bisque is a smooth soup, and turtle bisque is smooth because the turtle has given up everything it had to give. Turtle meat and soothing spice simmer through the afternoon, and then the whole pot goes through a fine sieve. Press it with the back of a ladle until nothing is left that a spoon could catch on. Skip that step and you have turtle soup, which is a different and lesser thing. Soothing, it calls itself. I would say patient, and I would be right.

## 1295805 Briny Seafood Stew

A stew of the shoreline, made from what the shoreline offers, which is crab and, to my lasting regret, murloc eyes. The eyes give body to the broth, as they do in Westfall stew, and I have learned to stop arguing with them. The tender crab meat goes in last and cooks only in the heat of the broth, which keeps it sweet. Hot spice throughout. The result is briny and rich and smells of the sea before anyone has cleaned it up.

## 15906 Dragonbreath Chili

This dish began as a dare in some tavern no one admits to drinking in. It has outgrown its origins, as I did. Most cooks treat it as a contest and cook it as a weapon, which says more about the cooks than the chili. Mystery Meat is a name, not an excuse. Trim it as if it had a reputation to lose. Toast the spices before anything else meets them, so they wake up. The Small Flame Sac goes in last, pierced once, and you stand well back. You will understand why the day you do it wrong. Made properly, the heat arrives late and leaves slowly, like a good guest. Made badly, it arrives first and stays all evening, and the Night Watch is welcome to it.

## 15910 Heavy Kodo Stew

The kodos of Desolace grow larger than any in the Barrens while eating nothing at all, and their meat is denser than any I sell. I have no need to taste the cooking of the Horde, and I make an exception for this stew, because it is less Horde than it is kodo, and the kodo is blameless. Soothing spice and spring water, and a day's patience, on a fire so low it hardly counts as one. The meat goes in cold and comes out in threads. Do not hurry it, and do not lift the lid every hour to see how it is doing. It is doing what kodo does: taking its time. In Desolace they sell this recipe within sight of the kodo bones that litter the valley, which seems to me a little indelicate, and entirely practical.

## 20626 Undermine Clam Chowder

A chowder from Undermine, sold in Tanaris by a goblin named Jabbey who never has more than a single copy to sell and enjoys the scarcity more than the soup. The soup is better than the goblin. Zesty clam meat and hot spice go into ice cold milk, and the pot is warmed with great care, because milk takes its revenge on a careless cook by splitting. In Undermine they serve it scalding, to show they can. I recommend eating it at a temperature that does not require a healer.

## 18243 Nightfin Soup

Nightfin is a snapper caught after dark, which is how the fishermen explain their hours and their tempers. The soup is the fish and spring water, nothing else, and that is exactly enough. Simmer the snapper until the flesh slips from the bones, and take out every bone before the broth goes back on the heat. Keep the pot where a bubble breaks now and then and no oftener, and the broth stays clear. If it clouds, you boiled it. If it is clear, hold it to the light and be pleased with yourself, briefly.

## 1250145 Savory Turtle Stew

Turtle stewed rather than strained, which makes it a peasant's dish and none the worse for that. Turtle and a generous hand of soothing spice, simmered until a spoon divides the meat without help. Do not sieve it. The texture is the point of a stew, and a turtle stew has a great deal of texture, some of it deliberate. Serve it thick, in bowls, with no apology for being honest.

## 18245 Lobster Stew

Darkclaw lobster, from the cold shores of the north, stewed in spring water and nothing else. A lobster needs no help, only respect. Bring the water to a simmer and give the lobster to it whole. It is done when the shell goes red, and not a breath later. Shell it, and return the meat to the broth only to warm. A lobster cooked twice is an insult. A lobster warmed twice is a stew.

## 470359 Darkclaw Bisque

A bisque of darkclaw lobster made with holy water from Stratholme, which I am told is drinkable, though I have read the label twice and remain unconvinced. The expedition in Silithus sells it, and I suspect they invented it to use up the holy water. It works. The water is very clean and very soft, and it lets the lobster speak for itself. Simmer, sieve, and do not ask what else that water was used for.
